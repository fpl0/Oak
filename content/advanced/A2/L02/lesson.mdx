<Step id="S1">
The A minor loop of Roles & Registers, now in two sections. Same tune, same chords, same drums: only what plays around the tune changes.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Arrangers rarely change the tune to make a chorus bigger. They change how much plays around it. Before you run it, pick what bar 2 will do to your ear.

<Predict exercise="bigger-or-faster" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The form map draws the two sections; the roll draws every note in them. Look at both while it plays.

<Example id="verse-and-chorus" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>The verse has three parts: the bass once a bar, two-note chords, the tune. The chorus keeps them, plays them more often and adds a fourth, the strings pad high above the tune. How many parts play at once, and how they fit together, is the **texture**: here a tune over a bass and chords, thin in the verse and thick in the chorus.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
A full bar and a loud bar can pass for each other. Hear the same chord played two ways.

<Choice exercise="louder-or-fuller" />

<OnPass>Card B: four notes, four times a bar, sixteen sounds where card A has two. How many notes sound in a bar is the **density**, and it sits beside texture: one counts the notes, the other the parts. Card A was louder and still thinner. Turning a part down never makes it thinner; playing fewer notes does. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Verse and chorus play the same full arrangement, so nothing happens when the chorus arrives. Give the verse room.

<Editor exercise="thin-the-verse" />

<OnPass>Now the verse leaves space and the chorus fills it, though not one note of the tune changed. That is most of what an arranger does between a verse and a chorus.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Two ways to thin a section: take a part away, or let every part play less. Listen to the second half and tell them apart.

<Choice exercise="thinner-how" />

<OnPass>Every part was still there; the bass and the piano each played fewer notes. Both ways thin the sound, and they feel different: fewer notes keeps the colour and opens space, a missing part changes the colour.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Now the other direction. Both sections start thin; make the chorus the one you wait for.
