<Step id="S1">
Until now every drum you wrote was one hit: a kick, a snare, a hat, placed step by step. This is a whole bar of a drummer in one recording, kick, snare, hats and the quiet notes between them. Hear it as it was played, then cut up and played back in new orders.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The recording is one bar long, and the loop plays at its tempo, 96 beats a minute, so each quarter of it is one beat of the loop. Before you run it, pick what four pieces played from the last to the first will sound like.

<Predict exercise="last-piece-first" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Each piece carries everything the drummer played in that beat. Change the order of the numbers and the beats move with all they hold.

<Example id="look-inside" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Beat 2 holds the first loud snare, and putting piece 0 on beat 3 brings the kick of beat 1 back there. A recording played back as a sound is a **sample**: one drum hit, or a whole bar like this one. Oak made this break itself from public-domain drum hits, so it is free to use in anything you make.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Two numbers decide which piece plays on beats 2 and 3. Play the target first and listen to where the snare went.

<Editor exercise="swap-the-middle" />

<OnPass>The same drummer, a new groove: the kick comes a beat early, the snare a beat late, and nobody had to play it again. Cutting a sample into equal pieces and playing them in a new order is called a **chop**. `.splice()` does the same and also sets the speed of each piece to fit its place; `.loopAt()` fits a whole break to another tempo, and `.begin()` and `.end()` pick any stretch of a sample by hand. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The break on its own, cut into four. Count the beats and find the order.

<Choice exercise="which-order" />

<OnPass>The two middle beats swapped: a kick where the first snare was, and the snare one beat late. The loud hits are what your ear follows, so moving one piece changes the whole feel.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The tune over the break as it was recorded, with the band on bass and chords. Cut it up and play it back as a groove of your own. Eight or sixteen pieces give you smaller steps to move: a repeated kick, a snare a step late, the last beat stuttered.
