<Step id="S1">
A son band in C. The clave and the cowbell hold the two-bar frame; the bass and the piano each have a figure that lines up with it.

<Example id="son" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Start with the bass. It plays two notes a bar, and neither is where a bass usually starts.

<Example id="the-bass" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>The bass leaves beat 1 empty, plays the chord's 5th on the “and” of 2, and on beat 4 it already plays the root of the next chord, a beat early. That figure is the **tumbao**, the bass of son and salsa: each bar it pulls the harmony one beat forward, an anticipation in every bar.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Your turn: take a bass that sits on the roots and make it pull.

<Editor exercise="pull-the-chords-forward" />

<OnPass>Two notes a bar, and the second one already belongs to the next chord. Listen to how the chord change seems to arrive before the piano plays it. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now the piano. Its two bars are a pair, like the clave's, and the order of the pair matters.

<Editor exercise="turn-the-piano-round" />

<OnPass>Turned round, the piano lands with five of the clave's strokes in every two bars; crossed, it met one. A repeating two-bar piano figure that faces the clave like this is a **montuno**. This one is in thirds, and its last pair of notes arrives on the “and” of 4, ahead of the C.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
By ear. The notes never change; only the order of the piano's two bars does.

<Choice exercise="with-or-crossed" />

<OnPass>Facing the clave, the piano locks with the rimshots and the groove sits. Crossed, the same notes keep landing between them, and the groove pulls against itself.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The clave and the band's drums are given. The bass and the piano are yours: a tumbao, and a montuno that faces the clave.

<Make exercise="your-son" />

<CanDo id="cd.code.montuno" />

You can now layer a montuno and a tumbao on the clave, in any key, and hear when a figure crosses it. Next: the rhythm lab, where you take a groove like this one apart by ear.

<Recap />
</Step>
