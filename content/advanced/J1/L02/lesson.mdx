<Step id="S2">
A jazz chart writes the chords you know with a few glyphs: ∆ for a major seventh, – for minor, ø for half-diminished, ° for diminished. A symbol still tells you every note.

<Example id="the-chart" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>D–7 is Dm7: the dash is the m. And F6 plays four letters, the F major triad F A C plus D, the 6th above F: a **sixth chord**, warm and settled where a major 7th would shimmer.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Four glyphs, four kinds of seventh chord, each one read into code.

<Choice exercise="glyphs-into-code" />

<OnPass>The triangle, the dash, the slashed circle and the plain circle: a chart's sevenths read into code. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Bar 4 of the chart held a chord on C that hung in the air before C7 arrived. Hear it beside the plain C7.

<Choice exercise="float-or-lean" />

<OnPass>Card A plays C7sus: the 4th above C, F, sits where the 3rd, E, would be. With no 3rd the chord is neither bright nor dark, and it floats until the 3rd comes back: a **sus chord**.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Editor exercise="play-from-the-page" />
