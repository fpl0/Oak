<Step id="S1">
A ii–V–I in B♭, and a pianist who plays almost nothing. Listen for how much two notes a chord can say.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The same two notes a chord, held a bar each, with the chord band and the chord-tone lens open.

<Example id="held" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>The 3rd and the 7th, the guide tones you followed through a chain of sevenths in the core. B♭, the 7th of Cm7, falls one half step to A, the 3rd of F7; A stays to become the 7th of B♭maj7. Under it, E♭ stays from Cm7 to F7 and then slides to D. Two threads, and neither ever leaps.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Your voice is analysed live on this computer. Nothing is recorded or sent.

Sing the top thread and you hold the whole ii–V–I together with one note a bar.

<Sing exercise="sing-the-thread" />

<OnPass>Do, ti, ti, ti: the chords moved three times under you, and your note moved once, by a half step. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The lower thread starts on E♭. Play the target, then write the rest.

<Editor exercise="the-other-thread" />

<OnPass>E♭, E♭, D, D: the 3rd of Cm7 stays as the 7th of F7, then falls a half step to the 3rd of B♭maj7. You now have both threads, and between them they spell every chord.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The default dictionary plays every note of each chord. One call keeps only the threads.

<Editor exercise="only-the-thread" />

<OnPass>Two notes a chord, and the ii–V–I is still unmistakable. The guide-tone dictionary is a quick way to hear the threads of any progression, and a sparse, open comp under a busy tune.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The drums are in; the bass and the guide tones play. The vibes start on the roots, which say nothing new. Write a tune that lands on the threads instead.

<Make exercise="a-tune-on-the-thread" />

<CanDo id="cd.sing.guide-tones" />

You can now sing and write the guide tones through a ii–V–I, and build a tune that outlines the chords by itself. Next: a root under these two notes makes a shell, the voicing jazz pianists comp with.

</Step>
