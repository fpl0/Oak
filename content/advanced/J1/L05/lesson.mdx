<Step id="S2">
The left hand alone, one chord a bar. Read the lowest note first.

<Example id="shells-held" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Three notes: in bar 2, G at the bottom, then F, the 7th, and B, the 3rd. The 5th is gone, and nothing is lost: the root says which chord, the 3rd and the 7th say what kind. That is a **shell voicing**, the voicing jazz pianists comp with. Above the roots run the guide-tone threads: F stays and C falls to B, then F falls to E and B stays.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Now with your hands, on a chord that is not in the loop.

<OnPass>F, A and E: the root, the 3rd and the 7th of Fmaj7, with C, the 5th, left out. Either order of the top two is a shell.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The dictionary's voicings fill the middle of the keyboard. Replace them with your own three notes a chord.

<Editor exercise="shells-by-hand" />

<OnPass>Your own shells, comping on beat 1 and the and of 2. Each chord is three notes, and the trio sounds more open for it. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
How a backing like this one is made. The chord string feeds the bass and the vibes: `rootNotes` takes each chord's root, and `n` before `.set(chord(changes))` plays the voicing's notes one at a time, lowest first, so `n` 0 is the lowest note of each chord. The shells are notes you wrote yourself. Read it; nothing to change.

<Example id="one-string" views="code,roll" />
</Step>
