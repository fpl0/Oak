<Step id="S3">
Why does Dm7 sound like a stop at home for a moment? Read the roots on the roll.

<Example id="aimed" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>E, A, D: each root falls a 5th, exactly as D, G, C does into home. Em7 A7 is a ii–V aimed at D, and A7 is the V7/ii you already know. For one bar, Dm7 is treated as a home of its own.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The same move in B♭, aimed at Cm7. One chord becomes two; the bass follows, because both lines read the same string.

<Editor exercise="aim-at-c-minor" />

<OnPass>Dm7 G7 into Cm7: the roots fall D, G, C, and Cm7 lands like a small homecoming inside B♭. Because Cm7 is minor, Dm7♭5 G7 aims at it just as well. A ii–V aimed at a chord other than home is a **secondary ii–V**. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Another way to lean into Dm7, heard once and not yet written: one short chord between C and Dm7.

<Choice exercise="a-bass-that-climbs" />

<OnPass>In card B, beat 4 holds C♯°7, a diminished seventh a half step above C, and the bass climbs C, C♯, D. The next lesson puts this chord to work under a tune.</OnPass>
</Step>
