<Step id="S1">
A tune you have known since X1, slowed into a late-night ballad: Aura Lee's verse on the tenor. First over the sevenths of your U16 setting, then over chords from the last three lessons. Not one note of the tenor changes.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
One of the new chords, close up: bars 1 and 2, twice. Keep your ear on the bass.

<Choice exercise="a-climbing-bass" />

<OnPass>Card B: on the last beat of bar 1 the bass steps up to C♯, then to D. The chord on C♯ is a diminished seventh, C♯ E G B♭, slipped between two chords a whole step apart. That is a **passing diminished chord**: it adds no new home, only a climb into the next chord.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
A device fits under a tune where the tune agrees with it. Bar 3 shows where a tritone substitute can go, and where it cannot.

<Example id="bar-three" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>On beat 1 the tune's C rubs a half step against D♭7's C♭. From beat 3 the tune plays A and B, the ♭13 and the 7th of D♭7, and the substitute sounds at home. The tune decides on which beat a new chord can start.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now write the climb yourself, under the first phrase. Only bar 1's chord is yours to change.

<Editor exercise="climb-into-d7" />

<OnPass>Cmaj7 for three beats, C♯°7 on the fourth, and the bass climbs C, C♯, D into D7. The tune's C on that beat sits a whole step above the chord's B♭, a note it can carry. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The whole new setting again, by ear alone. Three of its bars hold two chords; one of those pairs is aimed at the chord after it.

<Choice exercise="find-the-aimed-pair" />

<OnPass>Bar 6: Am7 D7, a ii–V aimed at G7 in bar 7. Under it the tune holds D, the 11th of Am7 and the root of D7.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The verse over those sevenths again, with the drums. The tune is fixed; the chords are yours. Where the tenor holds one long note, a new chord has room.

<Make exercise="your-jazz-ballad" />

You can now reharmonize a tune with several devices and keep its melody: the move a jazz arranger makes on any old song. Next: a ii–V–I that travels through three keys in eight bars, then the checkpoint that opens your contrafact and your standard.

</Step>
