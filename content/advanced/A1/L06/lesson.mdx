<Step id="S3">
A wobble has two dials: how fast it goes, and how far it moves.

<Example id="how-fast" views="code" />

<OnPass>Six wobbles a second in bar 2, and the same small width in both bars. A small, regular wobble in a note's pitch is **vibrato**, as a singer or a violinist adds it: `.vib()` sets how often, `.vibmod()` how far, in semitones each way.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Match a singer's vibrato by ear. Too fast and it buzzes; too wide and it sounds seasick.

<Editor exercise="match-the-vibrato" />

<OnPass>The lead leans into its long notes now, the way a singer does. Vibrato on the held notes, none on the short ones, is how many singers and players use it: here every note gets it, and the long ones show it. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The pitch is one thing a slow wobble can move. Here are three others, one bar each. <CodeRef fn="tremolosync" />

<Example id="three-moves" views="code" />

<OnPass>Four dips in bar 2: the level falls and comes back with every beat. That is tremolo; in bar 1 a phaser sweeps the colour, in bar 3 the cutoff opens and closes. Each time a slow wave, too slow to hear as a pitch, pushes one setting up and down: that slow wave is an **LFO**. Vibrato is an LFO on the pitch.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Your ear for movement. One pad, one thing moving.

<Choice exercise="what-moves" />

<OnPass>The brightness: an LFO on the cutoff. The notes and the level stay put; only the top of the sound opens and closes.</OnPass>
</Step>
