<Step id="S1">
Seven and nine mixed twos and threes inside one bar. Six eighth notes can be cut either way. Here they sway in two big beats, the slow lilt of a 6/8 soul ballad.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Every other bar of the second loop, the chords break step with the drums and then fall back in line. Nothing speeds up, yet those bars move differently.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Follow the chords with your hands, not the drums.

<Tap exercise="tap-the-chords" />

<OnPass>Two, three, two, three: ten taps. In bars 2 and 4 the chords walk across the bar in even steps while the drums keep their two.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Two bars on the roll. Is bar 2 really any wider, or only cut differently?

<Example id="the-same-six" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Six eighths in both bars, at the same tempo: bar 1 groups them 3+3, bar 2 groups them 2+2+2. For one bar the music sounds as if it had moved into 3/4, and then it is back in 6/8. That regrouping of the same six pulses is a **hemiola**.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now the Rhodes alone over the drums, without the bass to help.

<Choice exercise="threes-or-twos" />

<OnPass>Threes sway with the drums; twos cut across them and make the bar feel wider without making it longer.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Same six eighths, same tempo: only the grouping of bar 2 changes.

<Editor exercise="regroup-bar-two" />

<OnPass>Bar 1 sways in two, bar 2 steps in three, and the drums carry on underneath. The regrouping works only because bar 1 stays in threes. Two bars in twos would just be 3/4.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Six pulses can be cut two ways, and you choose when to switch, just as you placed the long group of seven. Saving the switch for the last bar of a phrase is a classic move: Baroque dances often broaden the bars before a cadence this way.

</Step>
