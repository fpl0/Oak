<Step id="S1">
Six eighth notes to a bar, felt in two big beats: the slow sway of a 6/8 soul ballad. Same groove twice; only the Rhodes and the bass change.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Before you count anything, follow the chords with your hands.

<Tap exercise="tap-the-chords" />

<OnPass>Two, three, two, three. In bars 2 and 4 the chords stop swaying with the drums and walk across the bar in even steps, then fall back in line.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Now read it. The hi-hat shows the six eighths of every bar; the chords sit on some of them.

<Example id="the-same-six" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Six eighths in both bars, at the same tempo: bar 1 groups them 3+3, bar 2 groups them 2+2+2. For one bar the music sounds as if it had moved into 3/4, and then it is back in 6/8. That regrouping of the same six pulses is a **hemiola**.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
By ear. In each groove only the Rhodes decides the grouping; the drums never change.

<Choice exercise="threes-or-twos" />

<OnPass>Threes sway with the drums; twos cut across them and make the bar feel wider without making it longer. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Your turn. Same six eighths, same tempo: only the grouping of bar 2 changes.

<Editor exercise="regroup-bar-two" />

<OnPass>Bar 1 sways in two, bar 2 steps in three, and the drums carry on underneath. Notice that the regrouping only works because bar 1 stays in threes: two bars in twos would just be 3/4.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The band plays the drums, with the snare on the second big beat of every bar. Choose where your chords and bass cut across them: every other bar, or once at the end of the phrase, the way a gospel band leans on the last bar before it lands.

<Make exercise="your-hemiola" />

<CanDo id="cd.hear.hemiola" />

You can now hear six pulses regroup from threes into twos, and write it into your own groove. Next: threes and twos at the same time, in two parts.

<Recap />
</Step>
