<Step id="S1">
In the last lesson twos and threes took turns, one bar after another. Here they play at the same time. First each on its own, then both.

<Example id="both" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Feel it before you read it, with both hands: the left hand keeps the kick's two, the right hand taps the cowbell's three.

<Tap exercise="tap-the-three" />

<OnPass>Three even taps in one hand against two in the other. Together they make four places in the bar, nice, cup, of, tea, and only the first is shared.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Now read it. On the roll the two and the three sit in rows of their own.

<Example id="both-read" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>The two halves the bar, the three cuts it in thirds, and they line up only on the downbeat. Two even pulses at once in the same span, meeting there and nowhere else, are a **polyrhythm**: this one is three against two. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Your turn to write it. The kick and the bass hold the two; the cowbell is yours.

<Editor exercise="write-the-three" />

<OnPass>Three words in the cowbell's string, three equal steps, and they sound in every bar the kick does. Six would have landed on both kicks, and four on the kick's second note: neither pulls against the two.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
By ear now. The cowbell is the high sound, the kick the low one; count each layer on its own.

<Choice exercise="which-against-which" />

<OnPass>Three against two, its mirror two against three, four against three, and straight four against two. In the straight loop the kick lands with every other cowbell note; in the other three the layers meet only on the downbeat.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
No band: both pulses are yours, and the check listens to both. Write the three against the two, then make it a groove you would loop.

<Make exercise="your-three-against-two" />

<CanDo id="cd.code.polyrhythm" />

You can now play three against two in any groove, and hear four against three. Next: two patterns of different lengths that drift apart and meet again a few bars later.

<Recap />
</Step>
