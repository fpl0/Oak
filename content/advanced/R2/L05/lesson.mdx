<Step id="S1">
The same groove twice. The snare and the hi-hat never change; listen to the kick and the bass.

<Example id="drift" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Feel it before you read it. Let the snare keep the bar and follow the kick wherever it goes.

<Tap exercise="tap-the-wandering-kick" />

<OnPass>Eight even taps, three beats apart: on beat 1, then beat 4, then beat 3 of the next bar, then beat 2, then beat 1 again. The kick never changed speed. The bar line moved under it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Now read it. Four bars on the roll, and the code that made them.

<Example id="drift-read" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Three steps, played at four steps a bar: the cycle runs out before the bar does, so each bar starts somewhere else in it, and only after three bars, twelve steps, do the two line up again. A part that repeats a pattern of another length than the bar, over the same pulse, is a **polymeter**. Curly brackets hold the cycle; `%4` says how many of its steps fill a bar. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The bass already drifts. Send the kick with it.

<Editor exercise="let-the-kick-drift" />

<OnPass>Kick and bass now share one three-step cycle and land together wherever it goes. Four steps at four a bar, or three at three, would have been the bar again; a polymeter needs a cycle that neither fits a whole number of times into the bar nor fills a whole number of bars.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
One more way to move the beat without touching the tempo line. Listen for what the hi-hat does when the kick changes.

<Choice exercise="faster-or-not" />

<OnPass>The tempo line never changed, and each bar lasts 2.4 seconds on both sides. The kick took the same twelve hi-hat notes in threes, then in twos, and the beat you feel jumped from four to six a bar. Hearing the same pulse in a new grouping, so the beat seems to change speed while the bar stays the same length, is a **metric modulation**: here an implied one.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
No band: the line that keeps the bar and the line that drifts are both yours, and the check listens to both.

<Make exercise="your-drift" />

<CanDo id="cd.code.polymeter" />

You can now write two parts that drift apart and meet again, and hear a beat change speed while the bar stays the same. Next: three uneven hits spread over the eight eighth notes of a bar, the bass rhythm under son, habanera and a great deal of pop.

</Step>
