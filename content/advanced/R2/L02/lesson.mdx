<Step id="S1">
In the last lesson you wrote five and heard seven. This one writes seven and nine, meters you hear in Balkan dance music and in a good deal of prog rock and fusion.

<Example id="seven" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Each bar ends on its longest group, so it never settles: the end of one bar tips you into the next. With the clav on top it grooves like funk, in a bar one eighth short of four beats.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
A rimshot now marks each group start, and nothing else.

<Tap exercise="tap-the-starts" />

<OnPass>Short, short, long: two taps one quarter note apart, then a gap one eighth longer before the bar comes round. That extra eighth is what makes seven lean forward instead of marching.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Nine eighths can be bunched two ways. Run this one and see which.

<OnPass>Nine eighths, 2+2+2+3: three short groups and a long one, the long group last again. This is the nine of Balkan and Turkish dance music. Nine eighths can also fall into three equal beats of three, 3+3+3: a compound meter like 6/8, whose tempo line counts dotted quarters, here `setcpm(80/3)`. Both are written 9/8 and last as long. The grouping tells them apart, and so does the tempo line: this one counts eighths, `setcpm(240/9)`.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Seven and nine side by side, at the same speed. Count the hi-hat, find the long groups, then decide.

<Choice exercise="seven-or-nine" />

<OnPass>In both meters the bar is made of twos and threes, and where the threes sit is what you hear.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Now write nine. The tempo line is ready; the kick still counts seven.

<Editor exercise="seven-into-nine" />

<OnPass>Three short groups and a long one, at the same 240 eighths a minute: the bar is two eighths longer than seven, and every eighth is as long as before. Put `setcpm(240/7)` back and the same nine steps rush by at over 300 eighths a minute.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Last lesson you heard seven. Now you write it, and your accents decide where its weight falls. The drums, the bass and the chords are all in your code. Give the bar its accents first; a clav riff on the group starts then turns it into the odd-meter funk this lesson opened with.
