<Step id="S1">
In the last lesson you wrote five and heard seven. This one writes seven and nine, the meters of Balkan dance music and of a good deal of prog rock and fusion. Both put their long group last, where it pulls the bar round to the next downbeat.

<Example id="seven" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Feel it before you count it. The rimshot plays only where a group starts.

<Tap exercise="tap-the-starts" />

<OnPass>Short, short, long: two taps one quarter note apart, then a gap one eighth longer before the bar comes round. That extra eighth is what makes seven lean forward instead of marching.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Nine eighths can be bunched two ways. Run this one and see which.

<OnPass>Nine eighths, 2+2+2+3: three short groups and a long one, the long group last again. This is the nine of Balkan and Turkish dance music. Nine eighths can also fall into three equal beats of three, 3+3+3, a compound meter like 6/8 whose tempo line counts dotted quarters: at this speed `setcpm(80/3)`, 80 a minute, three to the bar. Both are written 9/8 on paper and last as long; the grouping tells them apart, and so does what the tempo line counts: this one counts eighths, `setcpm(240/9)`.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Seven and nine side by side, at the same speed. Count the hi-hat, find the long groups, then decide.

<Choice exercise="seven-or-nine" />

<OnPass>In both meters the bar is made of twos and threes, and where the threes sit is what you hear. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Now write nine. The tempo line is ready; the kick still counts seven.

<Editor exercise="seven-into-nine" />

<OnPass>Three short groups and a long one, at the same 240 eighths a minute: the bar is two eighths longer than seven, and every eighth is as long as before. Put `setcpm(240/7)` back and the same nine steps rush by at over 300 eighths a minute.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Back to seven, and this time it is yours. The drums, the bass and the chords are all in your code: give the bar its accents, then build the groove around them.

<Make exercise="your-groove-in-seven" />

You can now hear how a groove in 7/8 or 9/8 is grouped, and write grooves in both with the long group where you want it. Next: six pulses that regroup, from threes into twos and back.

</Step>
