<Step id="S1">
Every groove you have written so far split its bar into equal beats, in twos or in threes. This one cannot. Same groove twice: listen to where it leans.

<Example id="five" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Before you count anything, feel where each group begins.

<Tap exercise="tap-the-group-starts" />

<OnPass>Two taps a bar, three beats apart and then two: a long group and a short one. The bar does not split down the middle, and that is why it seems to limp and then catch itself.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Now read it. The roll shows one bar; the code shows how Strudel was told to play it.

<Example id="five-read" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Five beats, three and then two. A bar that cannot split into equal beats of two or three is in an **odd meter**: here 5/4, five quarter notes. How its beats are bunched, 3+2 or 2+3, is its **grouping**, and the grouping decides where it feels heavy. The tempo line counts what the bar counts: `setcpm(150/5)` is 150 quarter notes a minute, five to the bar, so one cycle is still one bar.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Seven is made the same way, only faster: seven eighth notes, in two short groups and one long. The order of the groups is what you hear.

<Choice exercise="seven-by-ear" />

<OnPass>Seven eighths come as 2+2+3, 2+3+2 or 3+2+2. The tempo line counts eighths there: `setcpm(240/7)` is 240 eighths a minute, seven to the bar. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Back to five. The tempo line already counts the new bar; the kick still thinks in four.

<Editor exercise="four-to-five" />

<OnPass>Divide by the beats in the bar, and the tempo stays 150; put the kick only where a group begins, and the bar falls into three and two. Under `setcpm(150/4)` the same five steps would have rushed by at 187.5 beats a minute.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
No band in five: the drums, the bass and the chords are all yours. Give the bar its groups, then make it a groove you would loop.

<Make exercise="your-bar-of-five" />

You can now write and count a groove in 5/4, set the tempo line of any odd meter, and hear how a bar of seven is grouped. Next: grooves in seven and nine that put the long group last.

</Step>
