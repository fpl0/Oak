<Step id="S1">
Every groove you have written so far split its bar into equal beats, in twos or in threes. This one has five beats, and five will not split evenly.

<Example id="five" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Each bar of five is a long group of beats and a short one. Swap their order and the weight moves: the same notes, a different lean.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Put the long and the short of the first loop in your hands.

<Tap exercise="tap-the-group-starts" />

<OnPass>Two taps a bar, three beats apart and then two: a long group and a short one. The bar does not split down the middle, and that is why it seems to limp and then catch itself.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The roll shows one bar; the code shows how Strudel counts it.

<Example id="five-read" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Five beats, three and then two. A bar that cannot split into equal beats of two or three is in an **odd meter**, here 5/4: five quarter notes. How its beats are bunched, 3+2 or 2+3, is its **grouping**, and it decides where the bar feels heavy. The tempo line divides by the five: `setcpm(150/5)` is 150 quarter notes a minute, so one cycle is still one bar.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Seven is built the same way, from short groups and a long one, only on quicker eighth notes. Where the long group falls is what you hear.

<Choice exercise="seven-by-ear" />

<OnPass>Seven eighths come as 2+2+3, 2+3+2 or 3+2+2. The tempo line counts eighths there: `setcpm(240/7)` is 240 eighths a minute, seven to the bar.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Back to five. The tempo line already counts the new bar; the kick still thinks in four.

<Editor exercise="four-to-five" />

<OnPass>Divide by the beats in the bar and the tempo stays 150. Put the kick only where a group begins and the bar falls into three and two. Under `setcpm(150/4)` the same five steps would rush by at 187.5 beats a minute.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Grouping five beats, counting them in the tempo line, hearing how a bar of seven falls: all three are new since this lesson began. No band plays in five, so the drums, the bass and the chords are all yours. The last unit's tools still work here: a pull into a group start, ghost notes in between.

<Make exercise="your-bar-of-five" />

<Recap />
</Step>
