<Step id="S1">
The trio in G, the same ii–V–I twice. Only one line changes between the two takes. Listen to what it does to the ride and the Rhodes above it.

<Example id="walking-trio" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The second bass line under the chords, held a bar each so you can read both on the roll.

<Example id="walk-held" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>A note on every beat, and beat 1 always the chord's root: A, D, G, G. Beat 3 is another note of the chord (E, A, B, B), and beat 4 sits a half step from where the next bar starts: E♭ into D, A♭ into G, F♯ into G, B♭ back into A. That is a **walking bass**: the approach tone you met in U08 on every bar line, with the beats between filled by the chord. It is the floor a jazz trio stands on.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
A bass that already plays every beat, but runs through each chord and then jumps. Give each bar line an approach note.

<Editor exercise="lean-into-each-bar" />

<OnPass>Same notes on beats 1 to 3, a new note on beat 4, and the line no longer jumps: it walks into D, into G, down to the low G and back into A. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Three ways a bass can sit under the same chords. Count the notes in a bar first, then listen to the bar lines.

<Choice exercise="how-the-bass-moves" />

<OnPass>In two, the bass holds; climbing, it moves but lands with a jump; walking with approach notes, it never stops leaning forward. That last pull at every bar line is what makes a swing tune feel like it is going somewhere.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The Band plays the ride and the comping on Am7 D7 Gmaj7 Gmaj7. The bass starts in two. Make it walk, your way: up or down, low or high, half steps or whole steps into each bar.

<Make exercise="your-walking-bass" />

<CanDo id="cd.code.walking-bass" />

You can now walk a bass line through a set of changes, under any ii–V–I you write. Next: the turnarounds that bring a tune back to the top, and a walk through a whole jazz chorus.

</Step>
