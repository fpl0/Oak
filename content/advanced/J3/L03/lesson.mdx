<Step id="S1">
The same trio, the same chords, three times. What changes is when the Rhodes plays them.

<Example id="charleston-trio" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The second take again. Tap with the Rhodes, not with the ride.
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Now write what you tapped. The chords stay the rootless A and B voicings you already play; you give them a rhythm.

<Editor exercise="comp-two-hits" />

<OnPass>Beat 1, then the swung "and" of 2: that is the **Charleston rhythm**, one of the figures jazz pianists comp with most. The second hit leans toward beat 3 and leaves the rest of the bar to the ride. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The other figure: each chord arrives before its own bar.

<Example id="pushed-trio" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Each chord starts on the swung "and" of 4 and rings across the bar line into the bar it belongs to. The first time through, Cm7 would have begun before the music did, so bar 1 holds only F7, arriving early for bar 2.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Push the held chords the same way.

<Editor exercise="push-every-chord" />

<OnPass>Every chord a swung eighth early, held across the bar line. The bass still lands on beat 1, and the Rhodes gets there first.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The Band plays the drums and the bass. The comping is yours: two figures to start from, and any hits that answer the ride.

<Make exercise="your-comping" />

Next: the bass that walks under this comping, a note on every beat.

<Recap />
</Step>
