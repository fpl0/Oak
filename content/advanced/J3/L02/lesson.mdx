<Step id="S1">
A four-note tune on the vibes over Dm7 G7 Cmaj7, with the Rhodes harmonising every note from below. Two takes; only the Rhodes changes.

<Example id="drop-2-trio" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
One chord, two ways. Read them on the roll.

<Example id="one-move" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Bar 1 is Cmaj7 in **close position**: C E G B, all four notes inside one octave. In bar 2 the G, second from the top, went down twelve half steps, an octave, and the chord opened to G C E B. That is a **drop-2 voicing**: the same chord, the top note unchanged, the inside spread out.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Now with your hands, one chord at a time.

<OnPass>A3 D4 F4 C5: Dm7 opened, with C still on top. Three notes kept their place and one went down an octave.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The tune is on top of every close chord. Open each chord the same way and the tune stays where it is.

<Editor exercise="open-the-chords" />

<OnPass>Four chords opened, the tune on top of each. The Rhodes now reaches down past the middle of the keyboard and leaves room around the vibes. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
How high a voicing sits changes it as much as its shape. Three ways to play Cmaj7.

<OnPass>Card B is the drop-2 from card A two octaves down: the gaps that ring in the middle of the keyboard blur down there. Card C spreads the chord with a 9th on top, root and 7th low and the close notes high. A rule of thumb: keep 3rds above about C3. Low notes want wide gaps; high notes can sit close.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
A chord under every note of a tune, with the tune as its top voice, is how a big band's saxophones play a line together, and drop-2 is one of the voicings they use. The Band plays the drums and the bass. The tune is yours, and so are the voicings under it: build each chord close under its tune note, then open it.
