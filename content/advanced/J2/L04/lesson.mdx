<Step id="S2">
Three runs up a scale, each from its first note to the octave.

<Example id="three-runs" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>A Dorian is G major started on A: the same seven notes, so over a ii–V–I in G it fits the ii and keeps the key. G Lydian swaps C for C♯, the ♯11, a brighter colour for home. A scale chosen to fit one chord is its **chord-scale**: a place to start, not a law. Mixolydian fits the V the same way.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The same three jobs in another key. The chord band shows the chords.

<Choice exercise="name-the-scales" />

<OnPass>C Dorian, F Mixolydian, B♭ major: B♭ major started on each chord's root. Minor-seventh ii, Dorian; dominant V, Mixolydian; major home, major, or Lydian when you want it brighter. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
One scale for the whole line. Give each bar its own.

<Editor exercise="follow-the-changes" />

<OnPass>A, D, G: each bar starts on its chord's root, with the chord's 1, 3, 5 and 7 on the beats. One line of numbers, and it follows the changes.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Four more scales for chords you will meet later: Locrian for the half-diminished ii, the altered scale for an altered V, Lydian dominant for a 7♯11 (Mixolydian with its 4th raised), and the bebop scale, Mixolydian with one extra passing note between the 7th and the root. One bar each, run up from the chord's root.

<Example id="four-more" views="code,roll" />
</Step>
