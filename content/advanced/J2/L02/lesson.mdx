<Step id="S2">
The minor version again, with the chord band open. Read the V's symbol, then listen to the vibes' first note in bar 2.

<Example id="minor-trio-again" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>B♭: the 9th of A bent down a half step, and the sixth note of D minor itself. The V borrows a note from the key it is heading for, so it leans home harder. With the half-diminished ii and the minor home, that is the **minor ii–V–i**: the same pull as the major one, in shadow.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
One cadence, twice. Only the V changes.

<Choice exercise="which-v-bends" />

<OnPass>Card B's V holds B♭, a half step above its root A, an octave up: a dominant with one note bent a half step. That is an **altered dominant**, here with its 9th bent down. Jazz players bend other notes of the V too; you will hear them next.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The trio plays the bright version. Darken all three chords.

<Editor exercise="into-the-shadow" />

<OnPass>Em7♭5, A7♭9, then a minor home, Dm7 (or its cousins Dm6 and D–∆7): the same roots, every chord in the minor key's colour. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Four bars in any key, on Rhodes or piano. Listen for where it lands.

<EarDrill family="fam.jzz.ii-v" level={2} trials={6} />

<OnPass>Bright or shadowed, in any key: you can hear which ii–V a tune takes before you read its chart.</OnPass>
</Step>
