<Step id="S1">
A slow ii–V–I in E♭, twice: plain sevenths, then the same chords with more on top. The tune does not change.

<Example id="colour-ballad" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
One chord, two ways. Bar 2 adds one new note on top; the Rhodes leaves the root to the bass.

<Example id="nine-added" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>G, the 9th: a 2nd above F, an octave up, 14 half steps above the root. Keep stacking thirds and the 11th (a 4th up an octave) and the 13th (a 6th up an octave) come next. Each is an **extension**: colour on top of the chord that leaves its job alone. Fm9 is still the ii.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The V can wear other colours. Same home chord each time.

<Choice exercise="three-dominants" />

<OnPass>Card B, B♭13: its 9th and 13th are notes of E♭ major, so it sounds sweet. Card A bends the 9th up a half step (B♭7♯9); card C bends the 9th both ways at once and raises the 5th (B♭7alt). Both are altered dominants, darker and more urgent than the 13th, and both resolve home all the same. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Four long notes over four chords. Some colour the chord; one rubs against it.

<Choice exercise="colour-or-clash" />

<OnPass>9th, 13th, clash, 9th. A♭ over E♭maj7 is the 11th by name, but held there it sits a half step above the chord's G and grinds. A note like that is an **avoid note**: fine on the way somewhere, never somewhere to stay. Over Fm7 the 11th is a colour, because nothing sits a half step under it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The trio plays the ballad on plain sevenths. Give the chart its colours.

<Editor exercise="colour-the-chart" />

<OnPass>Fm9, B♭13, E♭maj9: the same roots in the bass, the same jobs, and a colour inside every chord.</OnPass>
</Step>
