<Step id="S1">
Twelve bars you know, played twice by the trio: first the blues of U11, then the way a jazz band plays the same form.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Both charts back to back, with the chord band open. The frame is the same twelve bars; some of the chords inside it are not.

<Example id="both-charts" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Bar 4 leads into bar 5 with a ii–V, Cm7 F7 into B♭7. Bar 6 climbs back to F7 through B°7. Bars 8 to 10 are a chain into the last line, D7 to Gm7 to C7, and bar 12 turns around to the top. Seven bars differ, and the form still lands where the blues lands. That is a **jazz blues**: one common chart among several. Some players put Am7 D7 in bar 8, or stay on F7 in bar 2.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Now write the chart down. The trio plays it again; one chord a bar, each with its kind of seventh.

<OnPass>F7 B♭7 F7, Cm7 F7, B♭7 B°7 F7 D7, Gm7 C7, F7 D7, Gm7 C7. Twelve bars you can now read at a glance. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
One string drives the comping and the bass. Rewrite it once and both lines play the jazz chords.

<Editor exercise="put-the-jazz-chords-in" />

<OnPass>The jazz blues from one string: the bass follows every chord, two to the bar where the chart has two. In bar 6 the Rhodes keeps B♭7's D and A♭, the guide tones B°7 shares with it, while the bass climbs a half step from B♭ to B: one note moves, and the chord tightens before F7.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Your own chorus. The trio starts on U11's blues with nothing on top yet; the muted trumpet is yours to put a tune on.
