<Step id="S2">
The same 32 bars, read this time. The chord band shows each chord as it sounds.

<Example id="read-the-form" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Four sections of eight bars, A A B A. Each A is a string of turnarounds in B♭; the bridge is four dominant sevenths, each one a 4th above the last and two bars long, the last one, F7, pulling home into the final A. These are **rhythm changes**, the chords of George Gershwin's I Got Rhythm (1930). The song as published runs 34 bars, with a two-bar tag on its last A; jazz musicians play the 32 without the tag. Half the bebop tunes ever written are new melodies over these chords. Players vary the second half of the A; the version here turns through E♭ and D–7, while the simpler chart of the jazz track's last capstone goes B♭ B♭7 | E♭ E♭–.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The bridge on its own. You met this chain in the core: each chord is the V7 of a chord in the key.

<Choice exercise="number-the-bridge" />

<OnPass>V7/vi, V7/ii, V7/V, V7: D7 aims at Gm7, G7 at Cm7, C7 at F, and F7 at home. In the bridge each one moves on to the next dominant instead of landing, so the pull keeps building until F7 lets go into the last A. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Everything but the bridge is written. Right now the form never leaves home.

<Editor exercise="write-the-bridge" />

<OnPass>The bridge in place: after sixteen bars of turnarounds the bass climbs D, G, C, F, two bars each, and the last A lands home. Played twice as fast, one bar a chord, the chain runs out at bar 20 and starts again.</OnPass>
</Step>
