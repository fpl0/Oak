<Step id="S1">
The blues taught you to swing the eighths. This groove swings something smaller. Same notes three times: listen to how the sixteenths sit.

<Example id="light-lean" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Before any numbers, feel the long and the short.

<Tap exercise="tap-the-swing" />

<OnPass>Long, short, long, short: the second hit of each beat comes late, on the last note of a triplet.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Now measure it. The swing call has two numbers: how hard, and which notes.

<Example id="read-the-swing" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>The long hit against the short one is the **swing ratio**. An amount of 0 is straight, 1:1; `.2` is a light swing, 3:2; `1/3` is triplet swing, 2:1; `.5` is hard swing, 3:1. One number sets it. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
By ear. Listen for the length of the long hit against the short one.

<Choice exercise="how-hard-it-swings" />

<OnPass>Light, hard, triplet, and the triplet feel again on the sixteenths, where each half beat is cut long–short instead of each beat.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The second number decides which notes are paired. Here it pairs the wrong ones.

<Editor exercise="swing-the-sixteenths" />

<OnPass>Eight slices a bar, so each half beat swings its own pair of sixteenths: the lean of neo-soul and hip-hop. The other lines here sit on eighth places, which sixteenth swing leaves alone.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The band plays the chords. The drums and the bass are yours, and every sixteenth is even. Choose your lean.
