<Step id="S1">
A funk groove in C minor on one chord, Cm7. Hear its three pitched parts one at a time, then together.

<Example id="the-machine" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Alone, each part sounds thin and full of holes. Feel where one of them goes before you read it.

<Tap exercise="tap-the-clav" />

<OnPass>The clav lands where the bass is silent: never on the one, twice in the last beat of the bar.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Now read it. Without the drums, the roll shows which part owns which place.

<Example id="three-parts" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Ten places, and each belongs to one part: the bass takes three with the kick, the clav four, the guitar three. Parts that fit into each other's gaps like this **interlock**: together they play one rhythm that none of them plays alone.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Your turn. When the clav copies the bass, two parts play one rhythm and the gaps stay empty.

<Editor exercise="clav-in-the-gaps" />

<OnPass>The clav now answers the bass instead of doubling it: two parts, one rhythm with more places in it. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
A third part has to find places the other two have left.

<Editor exercise="add-the-guitar" />

<OnPass>Three parts, none on top of another: the guitar's short chords fill the last gaps next to the beats, and the groove runs on sixteenths no single part plays.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The drums are given. Build the machine: the bass with the kick, the clav and the guitar in the gaps it leaves.

<Make exercise="your-machine" />

<CanDo id="cd.code.interlock" />

Next: move a whole figure to another place in the bar, and hear the difference between a rhythm moved and a groove that leans back.

<Recap />
</Step>
