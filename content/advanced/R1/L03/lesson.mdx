<Step id="S1">
Funk. One chord, five parts, sixteenth notes everywhere, and one place where they all agree. The new sound on top is the clav, a funk keyboard that clicks as much as it rings.

<Example id="funk" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Find the place they agree on before you read a note of it.

<Tap exercise="tap-the-one" />

<OnPass>The first beat of the bar: funk players call it the one. Everything else in the groove is free to move because that beat never does.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Now read the drums. The snare has two kinds of hit.

<Example id="the-drums" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Six snare hits a bar: the backbeat cracks on 2 and 4, and four ghost notes whisper on the sixteenths around them. With the kick pulling on the sixteenths and every part meeting on the one, that is a funk groove.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
A kick on every beat holds the floor down. A funk kick keeps only the one and pulls around the rest.

<Editor exercise="kick-that-pulls" />

<OnPass>The one held, and the kick leaning into the places it leaves empty: the bass and the clav still meet it on beat 1, and the rest of the bar moves. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The snare's turn. Ghost notes are felt more than heard: leave them out and the groove goes stiff.

<Editor exercise="ghost-the-snare" />

<OnPass>Loud on 2 and 4, a whisper between: the snare now moves the sixteenths along instead of only marking the beat.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
No band this time: the pocket is all yours. One chord, and everything lands together on the one.

<Make exercise="your-funk-pocket" />

Next: a bass, a clav and a guitar that fit into each other's gaps, so three parts play one rhythm.

<Recap />
</Step>
