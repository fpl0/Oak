<Step id="S2">
Before you read it, follow the figure with your hands.

<Tap exercise="tap-the-figure" />

<OnPass>The same three chords in every bar, with the same gaps between them. In bars 2 and 4 they start later.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Now read the move. One call at the end of the clav's line does it, with one amount per bar.

<Example id="read-the-move" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Half a beat later, the figure's first chord sits on the “and” of 1 and only its last lands on a beat: the same rhythm leans on different beats and sounds new. Moving a whole figure to another place in the bar is **displacement**.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Your turn, with a cowbell and a bigger move.

<Editor exercise="move-the-cowbell" />

<OnPass>Bar 1 on the one, bar 2 a beat later: one figure, two rhythms. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Moving a figure by an eighth or a beat changes the rhythm. Moving a part by a few milliseconds does something else.

<Choice exercise="ahead-or-behind" />

<OnPass>Behind: 26 ms late, a hair after the hi-hat. The rhythm is the same; the groove only sits back. Playing a few milliseconds behind the beat is playing **laid back**, and it lives between about 8 and 45 ms. Further than that it turns into a new rhythm: at this tempo, a thirty-second of a bar late is 82 ms, which you hear as a displacement.</OnPass>
</Step>

The drums and the bass hold the bar. Your figure moves inside it.
