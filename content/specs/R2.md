Five lessons on pulses that do not divide into the bars the learner has played so far (contracts §2.6). A bar of five or seven is made of groups of two and three, and the tempo line counts its own unit (R2.L01); grooves in 7/8 and in the irregular 9/8 (2+2+2+3) put the long group last (R2.L02); six pulses heard as two groups of three, then as three groups of two, is a hemiola (R2.L03); two even pulses at once, three against two and four against three, are a polyrhythm, heard only when both play together (R2.L04); a pattern of three steps at four to the bar drifts across the bar line and meets it again every three bars, and regrouping a steady pulse can make the beat seem to change speed while the bar stays the same length (R2.L05). Every rhythm is felt before it is counted: tapped, then coded, then read. The track opens after U13.CP and may interleave with U14–U17; R1 and R2 may be taken in either order.

It never needs R1's words (metric weight, interlock, displacement); when it echoes them it says the plain thing ("the heavy places").
- Feel first, then code, then read (the rhythm track's order): every lesson taps the new rhythm before its first graded code step, and reads it on the roll (group lines drawn by the meter) or in the step string after. Taps are never graded and never gate; their tests pin one right tapping and three wrong ones. Counting words: groups are counted by their sizes, "1-2 1-2 1-2-3" (the group counts of contracts §6.9), never by beat numbers across the bar.
- Meters and tempo lines (contracts §6.9, STYLE §8; `METER_GROUPINGS`, `groupStarts`): 5/4 counts quarters, `setcpm(bpm/5)`, grouped 3+2 or 2+3; 7/8 counts EIGHTHS, `setcpm(bpm/7)` (`setcpm(240/7)` is 240 eighths a minute), grouped 2+2+3, 2+3+2 or 3+2+2 (group starts of 2+2+3 at 0, 2/7, 4/7); the irregular 9/8 is `meter: "9/8i"` (NEVER `"9/8"`, which is compound 3+3+3 with a dotted-quarter tempo line), `setcpm(bpm/9)`, grouped 2+2+2+3 (starts 0, 2/9, 4/9, 6/9); 6/8 counts the dotted quarter, `setcpm(60/2)`. One tempo and one meter per piece (§6.9): an exercise in another meter declares its own `meter`, the lesson keeps one. No Band is written in an odd meter.
- The 4/4 habit is the realistic wrong answer of the unit: a bar of five written with `setcpm(150/4)` plays five beats at 187.5 BPM, not 150 (the `tempo` constraint, which reads BPM through the meter table, fails it and gives the specific hint of §6.9: divide by the beats per bar); seven eighths spread as `bd ~ bd ~ bd ~ bd` group 2+2+2+1. Every lesson pins one.
- Grading (AUTHORING §20.2, the corrections-pass standard): every required rule checks what is HEARD. A grouping in a code step is `onsets` on the accent line with `contains` the group starts and `excludes` the other eighths (`perBar`), plus the `tempo` constraint; a Make's grouping is `grouping-accents` (planned; accents at every group start and nowhere else on the eighth grid); a hemiola is `regrouped` (planned; some bars 3+3, others 2+2+2); a polyrhythm is `polyrhythm` with `minBars` (planned field `polyrhythm.together`: BOTH pulses evenly spaced and sounding in the SAME bars, so tracks that take turns bar by bar fail, the pre-flight's A6 counterexample `perc: s("<cb*3 ~>")` against `drums: s("<~ sd*2>")`); a polymeter is `polymeter-cycle` (planned). In code steps the same effects are pinned with implemented `onsets` (`perBar: false` reads absolute positions across bars, so a two- or three-bar figure is checked whole). For every graded step: the reference, at least three other right answers, the unchanged starter, and at least one WRONG answer that uses the right function: `setcpm(150/4)` in 5/4, 3+3+3 in 9/8i, the regrouping written in both bars (that is 3/4, no hemiola), `cb*3` in alternate bars, `{bd ~ ~ ~}%4` (a four-step cycle at four a bar never leaves the bar).
- Hemiola and polyrhythm are different things and the unit keeps them apart: a hemiola regroups ONE stream of six pulses, bar after bar (3+3, then 2+2+2); a polyrhythm sounds TWO streams at once in the same span (3 against 2 in one bar). A polymeter repeats a pattern whose length is not the bar's over the same pulse, so it drifts and meets the bar again (`polymeterPeriodBars(3, 4)` = 3: `{bd ~ ~}%4` plays 0, 3/4, 3/2, 9/4, then 3 again). Metric modulation is taught only as IMPLIED (contracts §6.9, §2.1): the same cycle, the same pulse, a new grouping; the tempo line never changes, and `cps` or a second `setcpm` never appears.

## R2.L01 Five & Seven

Author: **R2a**. Key E minor, 5/4, 150 BPM.

**Objective (syllabus).** 5/4 and 7/8 groupings; odd-meter tempo lines.

**The one idea.** A bar of five does not split into equal beats of two or three: it is a group of three and a group of two (3+2, or 2+3), and seven is 2+2+3 or one of its rotations. The groups decide where the bar feels heavy. In code a bar is still one cycle; the tempo line divides by the units in the bar, `setcpm(150/5)` for five quarters at 150, `setcpm(240/7)` for seven eighths at 240.

**Sound first.** A groove in 5/4 at 150 BPM, grouped 3+2: kick and bass on the start of each group, the snare closing each group, Rhodes chords that change every other bar. Counted 1-2-3 1-2, it loops without a seam.


## R2.L03 Hemiola

Author: **R2b**. Key A minor, 6/8, 60 BPM.

**Objective (syllabus).** 6/8 ↔ 3/4.

**The one idea.** Six pulses can be heard as two groups of three or as three groups of two. In 6/8 the bar is 3+3; when the chords and the bass regroup one bar into 2+2+2 while the drums keep 3+3, the music seems to shift into 3/4 and back without changing speed. That regrouping of the same six pulses is a hemiola.

**Sound first.** A 6/8 soul groove in A minor at 60 dotted quarters a minute: in the first bar the Rhodes and the bass play on the two big beats (3+3); in the second they play three times, every two eighths (2+2+2), while the drums stay in six. Then again.

```js
setcpm(60/2)
```

## R2.L05 Polymeter & Implied Metric Modulation

**Objective (syllabus).** `{ }%n`; metric modulation only as implied regrouping at a constant cycle (no tempo change).
