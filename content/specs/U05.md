# U05 Triads: unit spec

<!-- GENERATED from content/specs/U05.ts by `bun scripts/content/spec.ts`. Edit the .ts, never this file; content/validate/specs.test.ts fails when they differ. -->

Eight lessons on chords, built by hand before any helper: a triad is three notes of the key stacked a 3rd apart, written `n("[0,2,4]")` from the first lesson; its quality (major, minor, diminished, augmented) comes from its two 3rds and is heard before it is named; it gets a lead-sheet name and an arpeggio; it can be turned upside down (inversions, slash chords); its low notes are read on the bass clef; and a progression of triads is voiced by hand so each note moves as little as possible. That last check (U05.L07 `hand-voiced-cadence`, ≥ 0.9, unrevealed) is the gate for `chord()` and `.voicing()`, which U05.L08 then compares with the learner's own voicing. Ear-training stage D starts here (`fam.chd.quality-triad`, `fam.chd.inversion`). U05.CP follows U06.L01 in the default order.

## Conventions for this unit

- Chords are stacked by hand from U05.L01: `n("[0,2,4]")` over `.scale("C3:major")` (chords an octave under the tune, STYLE §3 rule 4). The rule is "skip a degree, twice": `[k, k+2, k+4]` is the triad on degree k+1. `.add()` (stacking on every degree with arithmetic) is U06.L01's; `chord()` and `.voicing()` stay locked until U05.L08 and appear before it only in read-only `preview: true` examples (U05.L04 S5).
- A chord outside the key's own triads (A major, C augmented, a borrowed note) is written in note names with an octave: `note("[a3,c#4,e4]")`. Chromatic `n` steps (`n("4#")`) are U15.L01's; the lint refuses them here.
- Chords are graded as chords: the `pcset` matcher (pitch classes per onset, plus the bass where the lowest note matters), never by text and never by octave (contracts §6.7). `[9,7,5]` and `[-2,0,2]` are the same chord as `[5,7,9]`; tests must include one such equivalent answer.
- Chord names in prose are lead-sheet form (C, Cm, C°, C+, C/E); in code, from U05.L08, the STYLE spelling (`C Cm Co Caug`). When both appear, show both once: "`Co` (C°)". `chord("C/E")` is an error (`/` slows a pattern): an inversion is a stack or a bass track (STYLE §5.3 rule 3). Typed chord answers use `text` with `normalize: "chord"`, which accepts C°, Co and Cdim alike, C+ and Caug alike, and slash chords.
- Words that wait: "Roman numeral", "I, IV, V" as names (U06.L02), "diatonic" (U06.L01), "function", "dominant" (U06.L03), "cadence" (U06.L04), "voicing" (U05.L08), "chord tone" (U07.L03). Say "the triad on degree 5", "the chords that set home". A grader detail that prints numerals (the `progression` rule's "chords go I V vi IV") is overridden in `feedbackText` (U05.L01 S8 shows how).
- Staff: treble in U05.L01–L05 (one chord a beat reads as a stack, a "snowman": line-line-line or space-space-space); the bass clef and the grand staff unlock in U05.L06 (`tb.feature.grand-staff`), and from then a `bass:` track goes to the bass staff by itself. Degree labels stay on (AUTHORING §6); syllables are available (U03.L08 on).
- Bands: `band.axis-4bar` with `tracks: ["drums", "bass"]` when the learner plays the chords over given roots (U05.L01, L04, L07, L08: the Band's bass plays 1 5 6 4, one a bar); with `tracks: ["drums"]` when the learner picks the chords (U05.L02, L03, L05); `band.two-chord-vamp` with `tracks: ["drums", "chords"]` when the learner writes the bass (U05.L06). A learner's chords never sound together with the Band's chords.
- Ear training stage D begins: `fam.chd.quality-triad` (U05.L02 seeds level 1, major against minor; level 3 from U05.L03 adds diminished, level 4 augmented) and `fam.chd.inversion` (U05.L05). - U05's "Play from the page" step is U05.L06 S4: a bass line shown on the bass staff only, written in code.
- Wave 4 shapes (AUTHORING §17) that U05 needs before its lessons can pass: `chord-qualities` and `chord-inversions` (U05.L03, L05 Makes), `staff-expect.chords` (U05.CP), `voice-leading.gate` (U05.L07 S5, U05.L08 S1), `step.gate-check` (U05.L08 S1). ## U05.L01 Stacking Thirds

Author: **content-lead**. Key C major, 4/4, 88 BPM.

**Objective (syllabus).** `n("[0,2,4]")`: root, 3rd, 5th.

**The one idea.** A chord is three notes of the key stacked a 3rd apart: skip a degree, twice. `n("[0,2,4]")` is degrees 1, 3 and 5 together, and the same "+2, +2" on any degree builds the triad whose root is that degree.

**Sound first.** Drums and a bass line (1, 5, 6, 4) from the Band, a tune on top, and under the tune a piano playing three notes at once in every bar: the four triads of the four-chord loop.

## U05.L02 Major & Minor
