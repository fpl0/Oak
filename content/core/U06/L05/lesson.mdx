<Step id="S1">
In a full loop your ear goes to the tune and the top of the chords. The line at the bottom is easier to follow than it seems, and it tells you more.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Your voice is analysed live on this computer. Nothing is recorded or sent.

Under each chord the bass sits on its root, so singing the bass is singing the chords' roots.

<Sing exercise="sing-the-roots" />

<OnPass>Do, sol, la, fa: degrees 1 5 6 4, the roots of I V vi IV. When the bass sits on the roots, singing it gives you the numerals.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Now loops you have not heard, not always in C. Listen past the chords to the bottom note, and find each one from home.

<EarDrill family="fam.har.bass" level={1} trials={4} />

<Callout kind="tip">Sing the bass note quietly before you press a degree. Your voice finds do faster than your ear alone.</Callout>

<OnPass>Bass first: four notes, and the loop is half named. That is a good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
A loop with its code hidden. Write only the bass, then switch between the source and yours at the same bar until they match.

<Editor exercise="take-down-the-bass" />

<Callout kind="tip">The bass is judged by its degrees and its rhythm, in any octave. If it hides under the chords, slow it down.</Callout>

<OnPass>Do, la, fa, sol: `0 5 3 4`, degrees 1 6 4 5. Under triads on their roots that is I vi IV V, in C: C, Am, F, G.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The loop you took down, on the grand staff: the chords above, your bass line below, its degrees under each note.

<Staff example="on-the-page" />
</Step>
