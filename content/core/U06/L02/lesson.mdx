<Step id="S3">
Every degree of a major key carries one triad. Run all seven, then home again, and listen to each one's colour.

<Example id="the-seven" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Major on 1, 4 and 5; minor on 2, 3 and 6; diminished on 7. The same pattern holds in every major key. So a chord can be named by the degree of its root, written in Roman figures: upper case for major, lower case for minor, a small circle for diminished. I ii iii IV V vi vii°: each one is a **Roman numeral**.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The seven triads on the staff, each stacked on its root. From here, the numerals can sit under chords wherever you see them: I under C, ii under Dm, vii° under B°.

<Staff example="seven-on-the-page" />
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The numbers in `.add()` count from 0, like every `n`. The numerals count from 1, like the degrees.

<OnPass>I V vi IV. In C that is C, G, Am, F; in G it is G, D, Em, C. The numerals stay the same, because they name each chord by its place in the key.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Numerals travel between keys. To play one, find its degree in the key you are in, then stack the triad.

<OnPass>Am: degree 2 of G is A, and ii is minor. The same ii in C is Dm.</OnPass>
</Step>
