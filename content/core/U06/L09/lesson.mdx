<Step id="S1">
In Hear the Bass First you took down a bass line. Today you take down the chords above it too, one layer at a time.
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The same loop again, now to take down. The bass gives each chord's degree; its colour, bright or dark, gives the case of the numeral. Each pass opens when the one before is right, and the chord band fills in as you go.

<OnPass>Bass 1 3 6 4; major, minor, minor, major; so I iii vi IV. In C that is C, Em, Am, F. You never had to name a chord directly: the bass and the colour named it for you.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Now in code. The bass first, then the chords, and switch between the loop and yours at the same bar until they match.

<Editor exercise="by-ear-bass-first" />

<OnPass>I V vi iii: C, G, Am, Em. The bass told you every root; the chords only had to be built on them.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
New loops, new keys. Take each one down the same way: the bass, then the colours, then write the numerals.

<EarDrill family="fam.har.progression" level={1} trials={4} />

<OnPass>Whole loops by ear, in any key. Bass plus quality gives the numeral every time.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The loop from step 3 is yours now. Make it sound like a song: a rhythm for the chords, a bass under them, a tune on top. The drums keep time.

<Make exercise="make-it-a-track" />

<CanDo id="cd.transcribe.chords" />

<Recap />
</Step>
