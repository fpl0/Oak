<Step id="S1">
A loop on 1, 6, 2 and 5, played on the Rhodes over an upright bass. Listen to the colour of the chords, then hear the same loop with plain triads.

<Step id="S2">
A triad is two 3rds stacked on a root. This stack has one more number.

<Predict exercise="one-more-number" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The hook's four chords alone, over the drums. Each is the same stack, moved onto a new root by `.add()`.

<Example id="the-four-stacks" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Four notes: 1, 3, 5 and 7 on the first chord, and the same shape on the others. The top note is one more 3rd, a step under the root's octave. A triad with one more 3rd on top is a **seventh chord**, named after that top note, the 7th above the root. Its kinds and names come later in the course; here it is a colour.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
One stack feeds every chord of the loop, so one change colours all four.

<Editor exercise="colour-the-loop" />

<OnPass>`[0,2,4,6]` on 1, 6, 2 and 5: the I vi ii V loop, now with a 7th on every chord. That is the neo-soul sound of the hook.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The 7th sits a step below the root's octave: B is one key under the C above it. A 7th is a 2nd turned inside out, so it keeps a little of a 2nd's rub, a softness a triad does not have.

<Choice exercise="three-notes-or-four" />

<OnPass>Four notes a chord: fuller, softer, a little unresolved. The triads sound plainer and more settled.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The same stacking works on the keyboard: on white keys a 3rd skips one key, three times over.

<OnPass>D, F, A, C: the chord of bar 3, ii with its 7th on top. Its 7th, C, is degree 1 of the key.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Now your oldest loop, I V vi IV, the one from Stacking Thirds. The drums keep time; in the jazz-soul flavour they lean back. The chords and the bass are yours. A 7th on every chord is the neo-soul sound; a 7th on only some of them gives a loop light and shade.
