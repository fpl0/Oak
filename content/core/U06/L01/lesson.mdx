<Step id="S3">
Bar by bar, `.add()` lifts the whole stack one degree higher, so it lands on every note of the key in turn.

<Example id="the-seven" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Every note of the stack moves together, so its shape stays "+2, +2": a triad on every degree. Adding 4 starts the stack on `n` 4, which is degree 5. The number in `.add()` is always the root's degree minus one.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Four numbers, four chords. Two of them are still missing.

<Editor exercise="finish-the-loop" />

<OnPass>`<0 4 5 3>`: degrees 1, 5, 6 and 4, the loop from Stacking Thirds, now one stack and one line of roots.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The same stacking works on the keyboard. Stay on the white keys: they are the notes of C major.

<OnPass>D, F, A: the triad on degree 2, the same notes `[1,3,5]` plays. Its bottom 3rd, D to F, is three half steps: a minor triad.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Not every D chord belongs to C major. Listen for a note from outside.

<Choice exercise="which-d-belongs" />

<OnPass>Card B stays inside the key: D, F and A are all notes of C major. Card A's F♯ comes from outside. A chord made only of the key's notes is **diatonic**. The seven triads you stacked, major on 1, 4 and 5, minor on 2, 3 and 6, diminished on 7, are the diatonic chords of C.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
