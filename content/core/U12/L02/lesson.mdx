<Step id="S1">
Two chords over one groove, twice. The roots never change: C, then F. The first time the music is content to stay where it is. The second time nothing wants to stay put. Hear the difference before anything is named.

<Step id="S2">
You have written both of these symbols: `C7` in the blues, `C^7` in your soul loop. They differ by one character, and the chords by one note. Guess which before you hear it.

<Predict exercise="which-note-moves" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Here are the two chords side by side, stacked by hand so the roll shows each note, with Fmaj7 after them.

<Example id="half-step" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Bar 2. Cmaj7 and C7 share C, E and G; the fourth note is B in one and B♭ in the other, a half step apart. With B on top the chord rests. With B♭ it leans, and it leans toward the chord a 5th below: C7 into F, the way G7 leans into C.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
A major seventh chord cannot go just anywhere in a key. Here is the loop from Major 7 & Minor 7 with one of them in the wrong place.

<Editor exercise="one-note-from-outside" />

<OnPass>`G7`: G, B, D and F. On G the key's own notes make a dominant seventh, never a major seventh chord: its 7th is F, a whole step under the next G. That is the chord that leans home to C. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
On screen the difference is one character. By ear it is one half step at the top of the chord, and a different mood: one could end a song, the other could not.

<Choice exercise="rest-or-lean" />

<OnPass>Rest or lean. A half step under the octave, the 7th settles into the chord; a whole step under, it pulls the chord onward. Oak will bring this pair back in your warm-ups, and the minor seventh chord will join it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Your voice is analysed live on this computer. Nothing is recorded or sent.

The two 7ths, one after the other, against the same low C. Singing them puts the half step in your own throat.

<Sing exercise="ti-then-te" />

<OnPass>Do, ti, do: ti is so close under do that it barely leaves. Do, te, do: te sits a whole step under, and it would sooner fall on to la than climb back. That pull is C7 leaning into F.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
The drums are in. Your four bars are plain triads, and the tune already reaches for the 7ths. Decide where the music should rest and where it should lean.

<Make exercise="rest-and-lean" />

<Callout kind="tip">The tune is in note names today, so it can reach any key on the keyboard. Over C7, change bar 2's held `a4` to `bb4`, the chord's own 7th; bar 3 lands back on `a4` under Fmaj7. Over G7, hold `f4`: it falls to the `e4` that starts the loop again.</Callout>
