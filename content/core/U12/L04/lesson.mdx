<Step id="S3">
One stack, moved up the key a degree at a time. You never name a chord in the code; the key names each one for you.

<Example id="seven-stacks" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Imaj7, ii7, iii7, IVmaj7, V7, vi7, viiø7. Two major sevenths, on 1 and 4; three minor sevenths, on 2, 3 and 6; the half-diminished on 7. And one dominant seventh, only on 5. So inside a major key, a dominant seventh points home: the key's 1 is a 4th above its root. The blues bends that rule on purpose, with a dominant seventh on every chord, home included.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The loop you named in Major 7 & Minor 7, now as numerals. The case of each numeral comes from its triad, the ending from its seventh.

<OnPass>Imaj7, vi7, ii7, V7: home, the minor chord on 6, the chord before V, and V7 leaning back home. You can read any loop in a key this way now. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
No code for the tune and no roll: only the page, its first chord, and four numerals. The key signature tells you where degree 1 is.

<Editor exercise="play-from-the-page" />

<OnPass>Gmaj7, Am7, Bm7, Cmaj7: the staircase of C, moved to G. The numerals did not change, only the letters, and the tune's F♯ now sits inside Bm7.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The same seven chords in other keys, by ear. Hear the bass first; once you know the degree, the key tells you the kind of seventh.

<EarDrill family="fam.har.progression" level={5} trials={3} />

<OnPass>Bass first, then the degree, then the kind. With sevenths the numeral carries one more fact, and the key has already decided it.</OnPass>
</Step>
