<Step id="S1">
The seventh chords you know so far are warm, mellow or restless. Here are the two dark ones. The first opens a slow turn into A minor that you have heard under many sad ballads; the second is a single bar in a brighter loop, gone almost before you notice it.

</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The first chord of that turn is written like a chord you already use, with two more characters on the end. Guess what they do before you hear it.

<Predict exercise="what-b5-lowers" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Here is that chord stacked by hand from its root, and next to it the same stack with the top note lowered once more.

<Example id="two-stacks" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Bar 1 is B D F A: the diminished triad with a ♭7 on top. That is a **half-diminished seventh**, written Bm7♭5 and in code `Bm7b5`: Bm7 with its 5th lowered. Bar 2 lowers the 7th once more, to A♭, and now every note is three half steps from the next. That is a **diminished seventh**, B°7, in code `Bo7`. Neither can stay where it is. The first leads into E7 and on to A minor; the second pulls up a half step, B°7 into C.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now build the first one with your hands. A diminished triad, then the same top note Bm7 has.

<OnPass>B, D, F and A: Bm7♭5, all white keys. It is the seventh chord that A minor's own notes build on degree 2. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Back in code. The loop below is the turn from the start of the lesson with one wrong note in its first chord, and you can hear the tune rub against it. Play the target first.

<Editor exercise="the-keys-own-chord" />

<OnPass>`Bm7b5`, `E7`, `Am7`. Every note of the first chord now belongs to A minor, and the F in the tune sits inside it. Half-diminished, then a dominant seventh, then the minor home chord: this is how a minor key comes home in a great many songs.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
By ear the question is the 5th. The first two chords stand on the same root, so you can hear that one note sink.

<Choice exercise="calm-or-hollow" />

<OnPass>A minor triad underneath: a minor seventh chord, calm. A diminished triad underneath: half-diminished, with a tritone between its root and its 5th. That tritone is what you hear as the ache.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
The drums are in, the piano marks the beats and the strings carry the tune: a torch song, except that every chord in it is one you could have written last week. Choose where it should ache.

<Make exercise="your-dark-turn" />

<Callout kind="tip">Over E7 and over G♯°7 the tune can sing G♯, the raised 7 of harmonic minor: `n` counts from 0, so degree 7 is `n` 6. Keep it off Am7, where the chord has G.</Callout>
