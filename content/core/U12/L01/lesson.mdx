<Step id="S3">
Two chords, each a triad you know with one more note on top. Here they are stacked by hand from the root, so the roll shows them bottom to top.

<Example id="two-kinds" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>C E G B: the C major triad with B on top, eleven half steps above C and one below the next C. That is a **major seventh chord**, Cmaj7, written `C^7` in code: soft, settled, happy to stay. A C E G: the A minor triad with a ♭7 on top, a **minor seventh chord**, Am7: mellow, less sad than the plain minor triad.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now build one with your hands. Same recipe as Cmaj7, on F.

<OnPass>F, A, C and E: Fmaj7. All white keys, the E tucked right under the next F. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Back in code. Play the target first, then give the three plain chords their sevenths.

<Editor exercise="name-the-sevenths" />

<OnPass>`C^7`, `Am7`, `Dm7`, `G7`. On paper the first is Cmaj7; Strudel reads `^` for "major seventh". The loop is the one you stacked by hand in Seventh Colour, and now you can call every chord in it by name.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
In code the kind is in the name. By ear, listen under the top note: is the triad inside the chord major or minor?

<Choice exercise="major-seven-or-minor-seven" />

<OnPass>Major triad underneath: Cmaj7's kind. Minor triad underneath: Am7's kind. Tomorrow's pair is harder, because both have a major triad inside.</OnPass>
</Step>
