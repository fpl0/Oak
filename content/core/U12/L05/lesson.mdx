<Step id="S3">
Four bars of the cycle with the line on top. The chord-tone lens marks which member of each chord every note is.

<Example id="first-four" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>3rd, 7th, 3rd, 7th. F is the 3rd of Dm7 and stays to become the 7th of G7; that 7th falls one half step to E, the 3rd of Cmaj7, which stays to become the 7th of Fmaj7. The 3rd and the 7th of a chord are its **guide tones**: they say what kind of chord it is, and from chord to chord they stay put or move by a step, never a leap.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
A minor has seven sevenths too, the same seven as C major counted from A, with E7 borrowed from harmonic minor as in Raise the Seventh.

<OnPass>i7, iv7, iiø7, V7. Home is a minor seventh chord now, the half-diminished sits on 2, and the raised 7, G♯, gives V its dominant seventh. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The last four bars of the cycle. The thread starts on D; play the target, then finish it.

<Editor exercise="finish-the-thread" />

<OnPass>D, D, C, C. D is the 3rd of Bm7♭5 and the 7th of E7; it falls one step to C, the 3rd of Am7, and rests there. Two notes have carried the whole cycle home.</OnPass>
</Step>
