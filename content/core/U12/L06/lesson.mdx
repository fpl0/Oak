<Step id="S3">
Bar 1 is Cmaj7 stacked by hand from C, as you first met it in this unit. Bar 2 is the same chord from the left hand. Compare them on the roll.

<Example id="stack-then-left" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>The hand leaves out the root and lets the bass play it. That is a **rootless voicing**: the 3rd and the 7th, plus one or two notes that colour them. In its place the hand adds D, an octave and a 2nd above C. An interval wider than an octave is a **compound interval**: a 9th is a 2nd an octave up, a 10th a 3rd an octave up. That 9th is where the left-hand chord gets its shine.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The hand also turns intervals upside down. In the stack, B was a 7th above C; in the left hand B is the lowest note, under E and G. Hear each pair, then name the second interval.

<OnPass>A major 3rd turned over is a minor 6th; a perfect 5th turned over is a perfect 4th. Moving the lower note up an octave is an **interval inversion**: the two numbers add up to 9, major becomes minor and minor becomes major, perfect stays perfect. And C to the high E is a major 10th, a 3rd an octave up. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Back to the trio, in the voicing you know. Play the target first, then give the chords to the left hand.

<Editor exercise="voice-it-for-the-left-hand" />

<OnPass>One call and the chords move into the middle of the keyboard. From Dm7 to G7 only one note moves: C slides down to B, the 7th of one chord becoming the 3rd of the next. The bass carries the roots, so nothing is missing.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Five sevenths by ear, every kind you have met in this unit. Listen to the triad inside first, then the 7th on top.

<Choice exercise="all-five-by-ear" />

<OnPass>Five kinds, by ear. The left hand changes where the notes sit, never which kind of chord it is: you will hear the same five in every voicing.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
The drums are in. The chords are held in the plain voicing, the bass is on the roots, a line sits on top. Make it a trio: left-hand comping, in a rhythm of your own.
