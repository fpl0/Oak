<Step id="S3">
That note is in the second chord too. Here is the triad on degree 4, on both scales.

<Example id="stack-on-four" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>G B♭ D became G B D: G minor became G major. The note that moved is degree 6, a whole step above sol now instead of a half step. Minor with its 6th raised is **Dorian**: 1 2 ♭3 4 5 6 ♭7, minor with a lift. Its own chord is the major IV, and i–IV is its groove.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Back to the groove, in D minor again. Two edits make it Dorian: the scale under the tune, and the chord that holds the 6th. Play the target first.

<Editor exercise="make-it-dorian" />

<OnPass>Dm7 to G7, i to IV, with B in the tune and in the chord. Two chords back and forth, and it never needs to go anywhere else. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The colour is old. Drunken Sailor is a sea shanty in D Dorian: no sharps or flats in its signature, home on D. Read it from the page.

<Example id="the-shanty" views="staff" />

<Editor exercise="play-from-the-page" />

<OnPass>A B C D, C A G E, D D. The one B in the tune is in bar 6, on the climb: that is the raised 6th, and it is what makes the shanty Dorian rather than minor.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Your voice is analysed live on this computer. Nothing is recorded or sent.

That climb on syllables is sol, la, te, do. Sing it over the held D.

<Sing exercise="sol-la-te-do" />

<Example id="the-minor-climb" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Sol, la, te, do: in minor that second note is le, a half step lower. La is the Dorian note; you have sung it since major.</OnPass>
</Step>
