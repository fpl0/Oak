<Step id="S1">
Until now every key you have written in has been one of two things, major or minor. Today you change one note of a scale you know and get a third colour. Hear it before it has a name.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The riff changed colour and the numbers in `n` did not. So the scale under them changed. Guess how much of it before you look.

<Predict exercise="how-many-notes-move" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The same eight numbers on both scales, from home up to home. Read the note names on the roll, then look at the staff.

<Example id="two-scales" views="code,roll,staff" />

<OnPass>One note: degree 7. G major has F♯, a half step under home; this scale has F, a whole step under it, and on the staff that F needs a natural sign: the one accidental on the page. Its name is **Mixolydian**: major with a ♭7, the te you sang in minor and in the blues, now inside a major scale. Major, minor and Mixolydian are all the same kind of thing: a set of notes around one home, each with its own colour. Such a scale is called a **mode**.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now build it yourself, by degree, starting from G major.

<OnPass>1 2 3 4 5 6 ♭7. Six notes shared with G major and one that is not. That one note is the **characteristic tone** of the scale: the note that tells your ear which colour it is hearing. For Mixolydian it is ♭7. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Back to the riff. It is on G major again, and so is the bass. Play the target first.

<Editor exercise="one-word-twice" />

<OnPass>One word in each line, and the F♯s became Fs. The numbers never changed: `n` 6 is still degree 7, and the scale decides which 7 that is.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The colour is not tied to G. Four phrases on other home notes: major, or Mixolydian?

<Choice exercise="major-or-mixolydian" />

<OnPass>A half step under home pulls: major. A whole step under home leans back: Mixolydian. You will hear that difference in a lot of rock and folk from now on.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
The drums are in. Your riff is on G Mixolydian already, but it never plays the one note that says so.

</Step>
