<Step id="S3">
The same six bars with note names on the roll. Read them as a ladder.

<Example id="ladder" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>One note a bar, every time: G♯, C♯, F♯, B and E each drop a half step in turn. Lydian is the brightest, with its raised 4; Phrygian the darkest, with its lowered 2. Major, Mixolydian and Lydian have a major 3rd; minor, Dorian and Phrygian a minor one. That is the first thing to listen for.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Your voice is analysed live on this computer. Nothing is recorded or sent.

Before you name colours you need home. Two loops: let each one go round, then sing the note it rests on.

<Sing exercise="sing-home" />

<OnPass>Loop 1 rests on A: A Mixolydian. Loop 2 rests on E: E Dorian. Both are made of the same seven notes, the notes of D major, with another home. Only home decides which colour you hear. The four modes of this unit can all be played on the white keys, on G, D, F and E. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Now the ear on its own, each phrase on a home note of its own. Ask the three questions in order.

<Choice exercise="name-the-colour" />

<OnPass>Home first, then the 3rd, then the one note. Every phrase you heard was a plain scale with a single note moved.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Two small tools before the vamp, both about where a sound sits between your ears.

<Example id="wide-vamp" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>The right. `.jux(rev)` plays the line as written on the left and the backwards copy on the right, so one riff fills both sides. One thing to avoid: a `.pan()` written after `.jux()` on the same line pulls both copies to one place, and the width is gone.</OnPass>
</Step>
