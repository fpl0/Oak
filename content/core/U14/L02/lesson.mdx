<Step id="S1">
Yesterday one note of G major moved down a half step and the riff turned into rock. A note that moves brings a chord with it. Hear the loop you know, then the loop with one chord changed.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
In major, the triad on degree 7 is the awkward one: diminished, never at home in a loop. Mixolydian lowered that 7th. Guess what happens to its chord before you hear it.

<Predict exercise="stack-on-the-seventh" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The whole loop as stacks of 3rds, each on its root. Read the chord band under the roll.

<Example id="stacks" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>F A C: a major chord, on the lowered 7th, two half steps below home. The band writes it ♭VII: VII because it sits on degree 7, the flat because that degree is lowered, upper case because the chord is major. G major has no such chord; G Mixolydian does, and I–♭VII–IV is its loop.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The hook's chords by name. Write them as numerals.

<OnPass>I ♭VII IV I. The flat sign is part of the name: VII without it would be a chord on F♯. You have the loop and its name, a good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
A second key, the same colour. The riff is already in A Mixolydian; one chord under it is still thinking in A major. Play the target first.

<Editor exercise="swap-the-chord" />

<OnPass>A, G, D, A: I, ♭VII, IV, I in A. The riff's G now sits inside the chord instead of rubbing against G♯, and the loop sounds like a band in a garage.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The same note is much older than rock. Old Joe Clark is a fiddle tune that climbs to te and turns back, in A Mixolydian. Take its first four bars down by ear.

<Editor exercise="old-joe-clark" />

<OnPass>Sol la te la, sol fa mi; sol la te la, sol. The top of the tune is te, G natural over an A chord: the same ♭7 that gives a rock riff its swagger.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
The drums are in. Your loop has home and IV but no chord on the ♭7. Put it where you want it.
