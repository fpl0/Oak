<Step id="S2">

<Predict exercise="where-the-snare-lands" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Three labelled lines, and a mute button beside each. Here is the same groove written another way, with `stack()`; Oak keeps one line per drum, so each part has a name you can mute, read and change. <CodeRef fn="stack" />

<Example id="stacked" views="code" />

<OnPass>The snare on 2 and 4 is the beat a crowd claps on.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The kick has 1 and 3. Give the snare the other two beats.

<Editor exercise="add-the-snare" />

<OnPass>Kick on 1 and 3, snare on 2 and 4: the **backbeat**. It sits under a huge share of the pop, rock, funk and hip-hop you have heard.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
One line can hold several drums, but separate lines are easier to hear, mute and change.

<Editor exercise="one-line-per-drum" />

<OnPass>Same sound, clearer code: every drum on its own labelled line.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Put the backbeat in your hands. Tap on 2 and 4 only.

<Tap exercise="tap-the-backbeat" />

<OnPass>Two and four. Once you can feel it, you will start hearing it in song after song.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
`.bank()` swaps the whole kit for another drum machine. The 808 and 909 are two of the most recorded machines ever made. <CodeRef fn="bank" />

<OnPass>The rhythm is in the words; the kit is in `.bank()`. Change one and the other stays put.</OnPass>
</Step>
