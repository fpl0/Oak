<Step id="S1">
Everything so far came in fours. This groove does not. Count along from the kick.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<Callout kind="tip">The band leans on 1 with its bass and answers on 2 and 3 with its chords: oom, pah, pah.</Callout>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Put the count in your hands. One tap per bar, on the kick.

<Tap exercise="tap-on-one" />

<OnPass>Every 1 lands on the kick, and three beats pass before the next one. That lilt, strong-weak-weak, is a waltz.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The drums line has three words, and the tempo line ends in a different number.

<OnPass>The number after `/` is the beats in a bar, and the words in the quotes share that bar. How many beats a bar holds, and how they are grouped, is the **meter**. Everything before this lesson was in four; this groove is in three.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
One more bar size, for contrast: two beats, `setcpm(100/2)`. Marching bands walk to it.

<OnPass>On paper the bar size is two numbers stacked at the start of a piece: 4/4, 3/4, 2/4. The top number counts the beats in a bar; the bottom 4 says each beat is a quarter note. That pair is the **time signature**. In code, the top number is the one after `/` in the tempo line.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
A drums line written for four, under a tempo line written for three. The words have to fit the bar.

<Editor exercise="make-it-a-waltz" />

<OnPass>Kick, snare, snare in a bar of three: a waltz at 108. Both halves matter: `/3` makes the bar three beats long, and three words give each beat one sound.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Now by ear alone. Find the kick, then count to the next one.

<EarDrill family="fam.rhy.meter" level={1} trials={6} />

<OnPass>Three or four by ear: count from one strong beat to the next.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Your turn. Three beats to every bar, over the waltz band. Keep the kick on 1 and the groove will sway with the bass.

<Make exercise="your-waltz" />

<CanDo id="cd.hear.meter" />

<Recap />
</Step>
