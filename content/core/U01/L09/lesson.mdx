<Step id="S1">
A new kit, the LinnDrum, and three versions of one groove. Play them one after the other; the band does not change speed.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
`.slow(2)` stretches a pattern over twice as many bars. It goes into the chain straight after `s("…")`. <CodeRef fn="slow" />

<OnPass>Same tempo, same band, but the snare now lands on beat 3, once a bar instead of twice: the groove feels heavier. That is **half-time**. `.fast(2)` does the opposite and squeezes the pattern into half a bar: **double-time**.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Copying a groove by ear is how drummers learn. Loop it, slow it down, isolate it, and write what you hear one drum at a time: snare first, then kick.

<Callout kind="tip">Count 1-&-2-&-3-&-4-& while it plays. Every hit lands on one of those eight words.</Callout>

<OnPass>Kick on 1, 3 and the & of 4, snare on 2 and 4: copied by ear. That last kick pushes into the next bar.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Turn the hook into its half-time twin.

<Editor exercise="make-it-heavier" />

<OnPass>Three lines, three `.slow(2)`. Every line has to slow down together, or the drums drift apart.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
One more way the same groove can feel different. Version B's hats are written with a tool you will meet much later in the course; for now, only listen.

<Compare a="hook" b="rolling" />

<OnPass>In B the hat between two beats arrives late, so each beat splits long-short. The groove leans back and rolls. You will write that feel yourself later in the course.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
