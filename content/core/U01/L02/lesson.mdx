<Step id="S1">
Four beats to a bar, over and over. The crash tells you where each bar begins.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Now tap only the crash. The kicks keep going between your taps; count them in your head.

<Tap exercise="tap-the-one" />

<OnPass>Beat 1, where the count starts again, is the **downbeat**. Drummers mark it with a crash, bass players with their first note, and listeners with a slightly deeper nod.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The `~` has been in your loop since the first lesson. Here it sits where a kick used to be. <CodeRef fn="~" />

<Predict exercise="which-beat-goes-quiet" />
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Watch the roll while it loops: three kicks, and a gap the size of one beat.

<Example id="gap-run" views="code,roll" />
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
One careful edit. The bar shares its length among however many words it holds, so count them before you run.

<Editor exercise="silence-beat-3" />

<OnPass>That `~` is a **rest**: a beat with no sound that still takes its place in the bar. The count goes on through it, which is why the snare on beat 4 still lands on 4.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Drummers rarely hit every beat equally hard. `.gain()` sets loudness from 0 (silent) to 1 (full). <CodeRef fn="gain" />

<OnPass>A louder downbeat is the oldest trick for keeping a band together: everyone can hear where 1 is.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Kick on 1 and 3 only: the floor that most pop and rock grooves stand on.

<Editor exercise="kick-one-and-three" />

<OnPass>`bd ~ bd ~`: two kicks and two rests, every beat accounted for.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S8">
Your turn. A sound on every single beat can feel crowded. Leave at least one beat empty and hear how the band breathes in the gap.

</Step>
