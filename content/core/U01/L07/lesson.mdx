<Step id="S2">
Until now a pattern in quotes played whole in every bar. Angle brackets change that.

<Predict exercise="one-per-bar" />

<Step id="S3">
`<bd sd>` gave one item to each bar: `bd` in bar 1, `sd` in bar 2, then round again. The hook's snare line uses the same trick with four items, each a whole bar in `[ ]`.

<Callout kind="tip">To hear a groove without one line, write `_` in front of its label, as in `_snare:`. The line stays in your code but goes quiet. Remove the `_` to bring it back.</Callout>

<OnPass>Bars 1 to 3 keep the backbeat; bar 4 breaks it with snares and toms and runs into the next bar 1. That break is a **fill**: it tells the listener a phrase is ending and the next one is about to start.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Four bars, one slot each. Play the blocks, then build a phrase that ends where a fill belongs.

<Parsons exercise="end-on-the-fill" />

<OnPass>The groove bars can come in any order. The fill only works last, right before bar 1 comes round again.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
A one-bar loop goes round and round without ever arriving anywhere. Give it a shape.

<Editor exercise="close-the-phrase" />

<OnPass>Three bars of backbeat and a fill: a four-bar phrase. `[~ sd ~ sd]!3` inside the `< >` writes the three repeated bars once.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Two bars this time. Hear them as a pair: bar 1 sets something up, bar 2 changes it.

<Tap exercise="echo-two-bars" />
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Your turn. Four bars over the band, and a fill to close them. Any line can change: the kick can push, the hats can drop out, a crash can mark bar 1.
