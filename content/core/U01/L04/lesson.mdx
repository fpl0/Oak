<Step id="S1">
The backbeat from last time, twice. Only the hats line changes between the two.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Same kick, same snare, same tempo. Play both cards as often as you like.

<Choice exercise="which-one-moves" />

<OnPass>Version B: two hats on every beat, and the groove starts to move forward.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Square brackets are new. Guess what they do to the bar before you hear it. <CodeRef fn="[a b]" />

<Predict exercise="count-the-hats" />
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Count the beats with a word in between: 1 and 2 and 3 and 4 and. The numbers fall on the beats; each and falls exactly halfway to the next one.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Brackets squeeze everything inside them into one beat: `[hh hh]` is two hats in the time of one. Splitting the beat into equal parts like this is **subdivision**, and the and is where the second half begins.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Most grooves keep the hats busier than the kick and snare. Give every beat two.

<Editor exercise="eighth-hats" />

<OnPass>Two hats per beat, eight in the bar. `hh*8` plays the same eight; the brackets show where each beat begins.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
A kick that lands on an and, just before beat 3, pushes the groove forward.

<Editor exercise="kick-on-the-and" />

<OnPass>`bd [~ bd] ~ ~`: the silent first half of beat 2, then the kick. Hear how it leans toward beat 3.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
No code now: count with your ears. The hats give you every number and every and.

<EarDrill family="fam.rhy.count" level={1} trials={6} />

<OnPass>Counting the ands is how drummers, bass players and dancers find their place in a busy bar.</OnPass>
</Step>
