<Step id="S1">
No new idea today. Ears learn distances the way you learned the sound of each key on a keyboard: many examples, each with its name.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<Callout kind="tip">While the pairs play, sing the name back under your breath. It sticks faster.</Callout>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Your turn to name them. The pairs climb, and they can start on any degree, not only on home.

<EarDrill family="fam.int.in-key" level={3} trials={6} />

<OnPass>Both notes placed, from anywhere in the key. The distance is the count between them, both ends included.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Going down sounds different from going up, even for the same distance. Guess first, then check yourself against the name.

<Callout kind="tip">A 5th down and a 5th up are the same distance. Your ear may take a while to agree.</Callout>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now name the pairs going down.

<EarDrill family="fam.int.in-key" level={4} trials={6} />

<OnPass>Down as well as up. That is a good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Your voice is analysed live on this computer. Nothing is recorded or sent.

<Sing exercise="sing-up-from-do" />

<OnPass>Do to mi, a 3rd; do to sol, a 5th. Singing a distance is the surest way to own it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Last, both notes at once, as in the previous lesson.

<EarDrill family="fam.int.in-key" level={5} trials={6} />

<OnPass>Up, down and together: the same distances, three ways to hear them.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
A riff, then an answer: the same shape, somewhere else. You can write bar 2 by hand, or let `.transpose()` move it by a name.

<Make exercise="answer-the-riff" />

<Recap />
</Step>
