<Step id="S2">
Musicians name the distance between two notes by counting degrees, and they count both ends. Try it before you hear it.

<Predict exercise="count-both-ends" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Home is always the first note here. Listen to how the second note changes the sound: close and warm, then open, then the same note again, higher.

<Example id="three-pairs" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>The distance between two notes is an **interval**, named by its count: 1 up to 3 is a 3rd, 1 up to 5 a 5th, 1 up to the 1 above an octave. In code, subtract: `n` 4 minus `n` 0 is 4, one less than the name, because `n` counts from 0.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The hook's second voice was a 3rd above the first all the way through. Give this riff the same companion.

<Editor exercise="a-third-above" />

<OnPass>Every note of `vox` sits two degrees above `mel`: a line in 3rds. Notice that you added 2 to every `n`, and the name says 3. That gap of one is the same bridge as degree and `n`.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Your ear can count too. A few chords set home, then home sounds and one note above it.

<EarDrill family="fam.int.in-key" level={1} trials={6} />

<Callout kind="tip">Sing up from do to the second note. The number of syllables you sing is the name of the distance.</Callout>

<OnPass>Heard from home, every distance is just a degree: 3 is a 3rd, 5 is a 5th, the 1 above is the octave.</OnPass>
</Step>
