<Step id="S2">
The very first loop of the course had a comma in its drum line, `"bd ~ sd ~, hh*8"`: the hi-hats played at the same time as the kick and snare. Now it is yours: one comma, two notes at once.

<Example id="pairs" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>B up to F is six half steps, exactly half an octave: a **tritone**. In C major those are degrees 7 and 4, and each leans towards a neighbour a half step away. In the last bar each one gets there, B up to C and F down to E: the two notes close in to a 3rd, and the pull lets go.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The ear sorts pairs into two kinds long before it knows any names.

<Choice exercise="which-one-ends-it" />

<OnPass>C with E blends and could end a song: **consonance**. C with D♭ rubs and wants to move: **dissonance**. 3rds, 6ths, 5ths and octaves sound at rest; 2nds, 7ths and the tritone pull. The 4th sits in between: open, but less settled than the 5th.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The page shows four pairs: two notes on one stem sound together. The code below reads them one note at a time.

<Staff example="the-page" />

<Editor exercise="play-them-together" />

<OnPass>Four pairs, four beats: at rest, at rest, a pull, and the release. That last move, a dissonance stepping to a consonance, is a small version of what makes music go forward.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Now the pairs come from all over the key. Hearing two notes at once is harder than one after the other; take your time.

<EarDrill family="fam.int.in-key" level={5} trials={6} />

<Callout kind="tip">Hum the top note first, then the bottom one. Then find each on the way down to do.</Callout>

<OnPass>Two notes at once, both named. Your ear is starting to hear inside a sound, not only along a line.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The pull you heard in the hook is not special to C. Every major key has exactly one pair like it.

<Choice exercise="where-the-pull-lives" />

<OnPass>4 and 7: in C, F and B; in G, C and F♯. When you hear that pull in a song, home is usually close.</OnPass>
</Step>
