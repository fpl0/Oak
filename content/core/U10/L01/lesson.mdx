<Step id="S3">
Two scales from C to C. Watch the roll and its degree labels while they play.

<Example id="two-scales" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Home stays on C. Degrees 3, 6 and 7 each drop a half step, to ♭3, ♭6 and ♭7, and everything else stays where it was. That is **natural minor**, major's dark twin. Sung, it is do re me fa sol le te: 1 is still do, and the three lowered notes take new syllables.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
A tune written in note names does not follow the scale word. This one still has major's letters over minor chords: hear how it rubs, then fix it.

<Editor exercise="darken-it-by-hand" />

<OnPass>E♭, B♭ and A♭: the tune now sits on its minor chords. Degrees in `n()` turn minor with one word; note names need their three flats. You have turned a whole loop dark, a good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">

Me and le are the two notes that make a tune sound minor. Le leans down onto sol.

<Sing exercise="sing-the-dark-scale" />

<OnPass>Do re me fa sol le sol. That little fall from le to sol is the sigh of a minor key.</OnPass>
