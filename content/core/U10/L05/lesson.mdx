<Step id="S1">

<Step id="S2">

<Step id="S3">
Run the first loop and watch the chord band name each chord in A minor.

<Example id="in-a-minor" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>i VI III VII: home, then the key's three major chords in a row, ♭6, ♭3 and ♭7. Natural minor has an older name, **Aeolian**, and this is the Aeolian loop, minor's four-chord song. Read in C it is vi IV I V, the axis loop started on vi: the same chords, and the tune decides which home you hear.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The loop in stacks, over the minor scale. Four numbers stand between it and the Aeolian loop.

<Editor exercise="write-the-aeolian-loop" />

<OnPass>0 5 2 6: Am F C G. You can write minor's most-used loop from its numerals, a good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Each card plays the same four chords. Listen to the start of each card and to where its tune rests.

<Choice exercise="where-is-home" />

<OnPass>A loop that starts on Am with a tune that rests on A is home on A: the Aeolian loop. Start the loop on C, or let the tune rest on C, and the same chords sound like C major: I V vi IV, or vi IV I V.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Your voice is analysed live on this computer. Nothing is recorded or sent.

The roots of the loop are the loop. Sing them and you can hear it without any chords at all.

<Sing exercise="sing-the-aeolian-roots" />

<OnPass>Do, le, me, te: three of minor's own syllables in four notes. With do on A, the roots alone spell out the minor key.</OnPass>
</Step>
