<Step id="S1">
A groove to live in for the whole lesson: Rhodes, a bouncing fingerbass and a tune that starts on one of minor's dark notes.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
No code this time, just your ears. Four chords set home in a new minor key each time; then one note.

<EarDrill family="fam.deg.fet-minor" level={1} trials={6} />

<OnPass>Do is rest, sol is open, and me, right above re, gives the key its colour. These three come back in tomorrow's warm-up.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
One note apart. Play each card as often as you like.

<Choice exercise="me-or-mi" />

<OnPass>Card 1: C over the low A, me, a half step under mi. One note decides whether a tune is major or minor. That is a good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Your voice is analysed live on this computer. Nothing is recorded or sent.

In major, ti leans hard into do. In minor, te sits a whole step below and arrives more gently.

<Sing exercise="le-to-sol-te-to-do" />

<OnPass>Le sol, te do. Le sighs down onto sol; te steps up to do without the tug of ti. That softer arrival is much of the sound of minor rock and soul.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Take a minor phrase down by ear. Switch between the source and your code at the same bar, and slow it down when you need to.

<Editor exercise="take-it-down" />

<OnPass>Me re do, then le falling onto sol. You heard each dark note against home and wrote it down.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The drums keep time and the groove plays on. The tune is yours: give it all three dark notes and lean on them.

<Make exercise="lean-on-the-dark-notes" />

You can now tell a minor tune from a major one and hear me, le and te against home; minor degrees join your warm-ups. Next: the chords of a minor key.

</Step>
