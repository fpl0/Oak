<Step id="S2">

<Predict exercise="how-many-black-keys" />

<OnPass>None. A minor plays the white keys from A to A: the same seven notes as C major, with a new home.</OnPass>

<Step id="S3">
Three scales, one bar each. The key-signature panel shows the sharps and flats of each.

<Example id="three-scales" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>A minor uses C major's notes exactly, with home on degree 6 of C: A minor is C major's **relative minor**. C minor keeps C as home and lowers 3, 6 and 7, three flats: it is C major's **parallel minor**. Every major key has both twins: one with the same notes, one with the same home.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The dark loop of The Dark Twin was this twin, before it had a name. Here is a new loop in C major. Hear the target, then move all three lines.

<Editor exercise="the-twin-at-home" />

<OnPass>Three words changed, `major` to `minor`, and home stayed on C: C minor, the parallel twin. The numbers in `n()` never moved. You can stop here for today if you need to; the loop stands on its own.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Any major key's relative minor starts on its degree 6. Count up the scale; no sound needed.

<Choice exercise="find-the-relative" />

<OnPass>D minor shares F major's notes, E minor shares G major's. On the circle of fifths each major key has its relative minor beside it, with the same sharps or flats.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Twins with the same notes share a key signature too. A minor key writes the sharps or flats of its relative major.

<StaffInput exercise="d-minor-signature" />

<OnPass>One flat, B♭: the signature of F major, D minor's relative. A minor has none, like C major. The minor signatures join your key-signature cards.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
The drums keep time. The loop, its bass and its tune are in C major and end on C. Give them the twin with the same notes and a new home.

Any major key now has two minor twins you can find, play and write. Next: hearing minor's own notes by ear.
