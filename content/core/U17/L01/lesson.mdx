<Step id="S1">
Two verses on the Rhodes over an upright bass. Both stop on the same note, a G. Listen to whether that G is a pause or an arrival.

</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Three of these chords are old friends in C. The fourth is the chord you met in Five of Five, the one that leans into G. Find its stranger before you hear it.

<Predict exercise="the-new-note" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The second verse again. The tune's `.scale()` holds a plan: six bars of C major, then two of G major. The chord band reads every bar in C.

<Example id="the-door" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Am belongs to both keys, and bar 7 brings F♯, which only G major has. In Five of Five the same D7 was a visit: the music went straight back to C. Here it does not go back. Changing key in the middle of a piece, so that a new note sounds like home, is a **modulation**. A chord that both keys own, used as the door between them, is a **pivot chord**.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The door has two names, one on each side of it.

<OnPass>vi in C major, ii in G major. In C, Am is the minor chord on 6; in G it is the chord before V, the one that usually leads to D7. That second name is why D7 can follow it so smoothly.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Play this one first and listen to bar 7: the tune has already moved to G, the chords have not. Then play the target.

<Editor exercise="write-the-door" />

<OnPass>C, Am, then D7 and G: the door, G's own V7, and the new home. The tune and the chords now agree on where home is. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
No code for the tune and no roll: only the page. It starts in F major, one flat, and the B♮ in bar 3 tells you the key has moved.

<Editor exercise="play-from-the-page" />

<OnPass>F, Dm, G7, C. Dm is vi in F and ii in C, and G7 is C's V7: the same door as in C to G, a 5th lower. Every move to the key a 5th up works this way.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
The drums are in. The verse you heard first goes round twice and never leaves. Send it somewhere the second time.

<Make exercise="a-verse-that-leaves-home" />

<Callout kind="tip">Am is not the only door from C to G. C, Em and G belong to both keys too: try Em before D7 and hear how the move changes colour.</Callout>

</Step>
