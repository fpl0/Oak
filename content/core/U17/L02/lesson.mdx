<Step id="S2">
In Pivot Chords a shared chord led the music into its new key. This time nothing prepares it. Here is the chorus tune alone: read its `.scale()`, then guess before you hear it.

<Predict exercise="two-keys-one-tune" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The whole chorus, twice. The bass and the tune are written once, in numbers, and their `.scale()` says which key the numbers mean. The chords are written by name, so the lifted bars need new names.

<Example id="chorus-lift" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>C G Am F, then D A Bm G: every chord, every bass note and every note of the tune a whole step up, two half steps, and no chord in between to prepare it. A key change that arrives without warning is a **direct modulation**. When it lifts a last chorus by a half step or a whole step, players call it the truck-driver's gear change. In the code, the numbers did not move. The key did.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Play this one first and listen to bar 5: the chords and the bass go up, and the tune is left behind in the old key. Then play the target.

<Editor exercise="lift-the-tune" />

<OnPass>`<C4:major!4 D4:major!4>`: one tune, two keys, each held for four bars. That string is the key plan of the line. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
A whole step is the wide lift. Some songs take the narrow one instead. Hear both before you choose for your own song.

<Choice exercise="half-step-or-whole-step" />

<OnPass>Card 2: C to D♭, a half step. Card 1 is the whole step you wrote. Both are the same move, and you can choose between them by ear.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Now the song from the start of this lesson, in sections. A key plan counts the bars of the whole song. A section inside `arrange()` starts again from its own first bar each time it plays, so a plan written inside a section would never reach its second key.

<Editor exercise="after-arrange" />

<OnPass>Bare numbers inside the sections, one key plan after `arrange()`: `<C4:major!8 D4:major!4>`, eight bars of C and four of D. The chords cannot follow a plan, because a chord name is not a number: the lifted chorus has its own section, `chordsChorus2`, with every name moved up.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
The drums are in and the whole song sits in C. Lift its last chorus: two key plans, four chord names. Then take it further.
