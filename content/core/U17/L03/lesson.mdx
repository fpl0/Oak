<Step id="S1">
You have changed key twice in code. Now the other side of it: hearing a song do it. The first test is the simplest one there is. Hum the first bass note, the home the piece starts from, wait for the end, and hum it again.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The two verses of Pivot Chords, one card each, in another order. Both start the same way.

<Choice exercise="home-or-away" />

<OnPass>Card 1 moves: D7 brings F♯ in bar 7, the verse stops on G, and C sounds like a stranger afterwards. Card 2 goes the same way for six bars and then comes home. That is the first question, and the one your ear answers before anything else: is the old home still home at the end?</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Your voice is analysed live on this computer. Nothing is recorded or sent.

Movable do means what it says. When home moves, do moves with it.

<Sing exercise="sing-home-twice" />

<OnPass>Do before, do after: C, then G. The second do is the same note the verse ends on, and nothing about singing it feels foreign. That is what a modulation does to your ear.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The second question: where. A lift starts the phrase again higher, and the bar where it starts is where the new key begins. Some of these pieces never change key at all.

<EarDrill family="fam.har.modulation" level={1} trials={4} />

<OnPass>Hum the bass, feel the jump, mark the bar. The drill keeps coming back in your warm-ups, and later it adds moves through a door. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The third question: how. A chord from outside the key is not yet a key change. The music has to stay where it went.

<Choice exercise="how-did-it-get-there" />

<OnPass>A visit, a door, a lift. D7 in piece 1 went to G for a bar and came home; in piece 2 Am opened the door and E7 kept the music in A minor; piece 3 rose a half step with nothing in between. Three questions, in order: is home still home, where did the first new note arrive, and was there a door.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The drums are in. Your song has a verse and a bridge, and the bridge sounds like more verse. Send it somewhere else.

<Make exercise="a-bridge-somewhere-else" />

<Callout kind="tip">Am is the door here, as it was from C to G: it is vi in C and i in A minor. When the loop comes round, the bridge's last Am leads back into C with no help, because C major owns it too.</Callout>

You can hear where a song changes key, and whether it used a door. Next: writing a tune down through its key change, in a pop chorus and in a ballad shaped like the standards of 1930.

</Step>
