<Step id="S1">
Your loop from last time, to set up by. Put your headphones on first: Oak plays to them and listens through the microphone, and the two should not hear each other.

<Setup part="headphones" />

<Callout kind="tip">AirPods and other wireless earbuds are fine for listening. Oak will listen through your computer's own microphone, so the earbuds keep their full sound quality.</Callout>

<Step id="S2">
Next, the microphone. Oak measures how quiet your room is, then plays a short sound to check that the headphones do not leak into the mic.

Your voice is analysed live on this computer. Nothing is recorded or sent.

<Setup part="mic" />

<Setup part="noise" />

<Setup part="bleed" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Every keyboard and pair of headphones adds a small delay. Tap along with the clicks so Oak can measure yours and take it off every tap you make from now on.

<Setup part="tap-latency" />
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Two last measurements: how low and how high you can sing comfortably, and how long your voice takes to reach the microphone. Oak moves every note it asks you to sing into the part of your range that feels comfortable.

<Setup part="range" />

<Setup part="sing-latency" />

<Callout kind="why">There is no right range. Low voices and high voices learn the same way here.</Callout>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
One note, then your turn. A hum counts.

</Step>
