<Step id="S1">
Yesterday's chords, back in four. Listen to the piano and the hats in both versions: the kick and the snare keep the same square beat, and the second one rocks like a slow dance at the end of a 1950s prom.

<Step id="S2">
Feel it before you code it. The count you said in 6/8, three to a beat, now turns up one beat at a time inside a bar of four.

<OnPass>Two bars echoed back, busy beats included. That is the whole idea of today: a beat can split in two or in three, one beat at a time.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
A new call, `.ply()`. Guess before you run it.

<Predict exercise="count-the-hats" />
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Twelve hats in a bar of four beats, on the roll and on the staff. On the staff each group of three sits under a bracket with a 3.

<Example id="threes" views="code,roll,staff" />

<OnPass>Three equal notes in the time of two is a **triplet**: the bracket with a 3 says so on the staff, and in code it is a bracket of three in one step or `.ply(3)`. Swing was made of triplets all along: the long–short pair of your shuffles is the first and the third of the three, with the middle one left out.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The hats already rock in threes. Bring the piano with them.

<Editor exercise="the-piano-in-threes" />

<OnPass>`.ply(3)` after `.struct()`: three chords to every beat, locked with the hats, while the kick and the snare keep the four beats square. That is the slow-dance ballad sound. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Reading triplets. A quarter note fills one beat; a triplet shares one beat between three notes. The tune is in C major from middle C, and `n` counts from 0, so degree 1 is `n` 0.

<Editor exercise="read-the-triplets" />

<OnPass>One step for each beat, and a bracket of three for each triplet: C, then E G C, then G, then E D C. In 12/8 the same bar needs no brackets at all, because there every beat already splits in three: a dotted quarter and three eighths, twice. One rhythm, two ways to write it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
By ear. The kick and the snare mark the four beats; the hats say how each one divides.

<Choice exercise="two-or-three" />

<OnPass>Two to the beat walks; three to the beat rolls. Tomorrow's warm-up keeps asking.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S8">
A ballad in plain 4/4, square as a march. Set it rolling in threes, then make it the last song on the jukebox. You can now write triplets and rolls, and you know what swing is made of; next, writing the rhythms of this whole unit down by ear.
