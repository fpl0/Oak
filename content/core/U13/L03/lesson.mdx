<Step id="S2">
Feel the ending before you count it. Hum it once under your breath, then tap it back.

<OnPass>Four even notes, then long, short, long: the last bar has only three notes, and the first one takes more than its beat.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
`@` gives a note a weight inside its brackets, as it has since your first drum loops. Here the weights add up to eight.

<Predict exercise="shares-of-the-bar" />

<OnPass>Three shares of eight: three eighth notes, a beat and a half. The C after it is one eighth, and the last C takes the other half of the bar.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now the same two bars on the page. Find the long first note of bar 2 on the roll, then on the staff.

<Example id="the-ending" views="code,roll,staff" />

<OnPass>The dot makes a note half as long again: a quarter note with a dot is a quarter plus an eighth, three eighths in all. A note written this way is a **dotted note**. In code there is no dot, only the share: `1@3` in a bar of eight.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Back to where the phrase begins, two bars on the staff. This is the first of the two changes you heard.

<Example id="bars-12-13" views="staff" />

<OnPass>Two beats: the E starts on beat 4 and the curved line carries it through beat 1 of the next bar. That curve is a **tie**: it joins two notes of the same pitch into one sound, across a beat or a bar line. It is also how a page writes the push of two days ago: the note arrives a beat early and holds over the beat it belongs to. The E is a note of the C chord that comes in the next bar, heard early. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Play from the page. Listen to the target first if you like, then read the lengths off the staff.

<LeadSheet example="the-riff" />

<Editor exercise="play-from-the-page" />

<OnPass>G for a dotted quarter, then A from the “and” of 2 to the end of the bar: on the page an eighth tied to a half note, in code one note with a weight of five. The riff leans because its long note starts between the beats.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
The other direction: from code to the page. The tie is a button and a key; the dot is one too.

<Example id="the-lean" views="code" />

<StaffInput exercise="write-the-lean" />

<OnPass>A dotted quarter, then an eighth tied over the bar line to a half note: the G belongs to bar 2 and arrives an eighth early. Every push you write in code has this shape on paper.</OnPass>
</Step>
