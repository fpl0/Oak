<Step id="S1">
Your seventh chords from the last unit, over drums and a bass. First they do what every chord of yours has done so far: change on beat 1. Then one line gets impatient. Listen for the moment the Rhodes moves.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Before you count it or code it, feel it. Nod on the beat with the kick and the snare, and tap only where the Rhodes comes in.

<OnPass>Your tap fell just ahead of beat 1 every time, and the kick answered it. That lean into the bar line is what you will write next.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The number inside the call is a share of the bar: `1/8` is one eighth note in 4/4. Guess what a smaller share does before you run it.

<Predict exercise="half-the-push" />
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Back to the eighth-note push, with the roll open. The kick marks beat 1 of every bar: look at where the Rhodes is when it hits.

<Example id="the-push" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Each chord starts on the “and” after beat 4 and holds across the bar line: one eighth note before it, seven after. A chord or a note that arrives early like this and holds over the beat it belongs to is an **anticipation**; players call it a push. Accents that land between the beats, against the pulse the drums keep, are **syncopation**. You have written such hits before with `.struct()`; now the whole line moves with one call. The first time through, bar 1 has no Rhodes until its last eighth note: the chord for bar 1 would have had to start before the music did.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Your turn to write it. Play the target first: the Rhodes gets to every chord just ahead of the bass.

<Editor exercise="push-the-rhodes" />

<OnPass>`.early(1/8)`: every chord of the Rhodes now leans into its bar, and the bass confirms it on beat 1. Same chords, same tempo, and the loop has started to move forward. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The opposite move has its own sound. A short chord after every beat is the off-beat chop of ska, reggae and a lot of disco.

<Editor exercise="after-the-beat" />

<OnPass>`.late(1/8)`: four chords a bar, each an eighth note after its beat, each still the chord of its own bar. Early leans into the next beat; late answers the one that just went by.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
By ear now. The bass is your ruler: it plays on beats 1 and 3 in all four grooves.

<Choice exercise="on-the-beat-or-pushed" />

<OnPass>On the beat, the chords and the bass sound like one hit. Pushed, you hear two: the chord, then the bass under it. The third groove pushed by a sixteenth note only, the tight push of funk.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S8">
Drums, bass and four chords, all landing square on the beat. Push the Rhodes, then keep going until the groove feels like yours.

<Make exercise="your-pushed-groove" />

<CanDo id="cd.code.pushes" />

You can now push a chord ahead of its beat, or lay one just after it, and hear which a groove does. Next: sixteenth-note funk, where every hit gets its own strength.

</Step>
