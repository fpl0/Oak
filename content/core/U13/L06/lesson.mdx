<Step id="S1">
Everything in this unit moved the hits: early, long–short, in threes. Today you write grooves down by ear. Start with one rhythm played three ways. The kick and the bass barely move between them; the hats tell you which is which.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The method has three steps. First find the beat. Then decide how it divides: in two, in two long–short, or in three. Only then place the hits. Here is step two on its own.

<Choice exercise="how-the-beat-divides" />

<OnPass>The kick could not tell you: in the swung groove and the one in threes it can land on the very same spot, the third of three. The hats always tell you. Two to the beat, or three.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Before you write a rhythm, put it in your hands. If you can tap it back, you can count it.

<OnPass>The hits on the click place themselves; the ones between the clicks are the ones to count. Each of them has a slot of its own, and slots are what you will write next.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
A funk groove, even eighths. The snare and the hats are given, so the beat is already found and divided: what is left is the kick. Count the slots, write the hits, then switch between the source and your code until they sound the same.

<Editor exercise="take-the-kick-down" />

<OnPass>Three kicks a bar, and in each bar one of them falls between the beats: the “and” after 2 in bar 1, leaning into beat 3, and the “and” after 3 in bar 2, right before beat 4. You heard a groove and wrote it down. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
A shuffle. The division is long–short, and swing is written straight: the kick goes in plain eighths, and one call per line does the rest.

<Editor exercise="take-the-shuffle-down" />

<OnPass>On paper the shuffle's kick is plain eighths; `.swingBy(1/3, 4)` moves each “&” to the third of its beat's three. Write it straight, then swing it: the same as you did for the blues.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
In three. A bar of 12/8 and a bar of 4/4 in triplets are the same twelve steps: four beats, each split in three. Here it is on the grid.

<Choice exercise="twelve-to-the-bar" />

<OnPass>The snare on the first step of groups two and four, the kick on the first and third steps of beats 1 and 3. Counted in threes, a slow blues or a soul ballad goes down on paper with the same three steps as funk.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
The groove you took down, with room for a reply. Players trade bars like this all the time: one plays a figure, the other answers it in their own words.

<Recap />

You can now write down a syncopated, swung or three-to-the-beat rhythm by ear: beat, division, then the hits, kick first. Next: a new unit, about the colours a scale takes when one of its notes moves; then the checkpoint on sevenths and rhythm, which opens the jazz and rhythm tracks.
</Step>
