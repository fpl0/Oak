<Step id="S1">
A funk vamp on two of your seventh chords: kick and bass locked together, hats and a clean guitar in sixteenths. Hear it twice. The notes and their places are the same both times; what changes is how hard each one is played.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The kick and the bass hit together, and two of their three hits fall between the beats, as in the bass grooves you locked to a kick. Feel that before anything else. The small snare hits will try to pull you off; let them go by.

<OnPass>Three taps a bar, two of them off the beat: the frame of the groove. Everything else you heard sits in the gaps around it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
`.velocity()` is a strength for each hit. It reads its numbers the way every control does: against the rhythm on its left.

<Predict exercise="two-numbers-eight-hits" />

<OnPass>Two numbers share the bar between them, half each: four loud hits, then four quiet. To give every pair of hits the same loud–quiet shape, repeat the cell: `"[1 .3]*4"` is eight numbers.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Back to the groove. Read the snare's rhythm string and its velocity string together, one step at a time.

<Example id="the-groove" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Beats 2 and 4 hit at full strength; the three hits between them, on the “a” of 2, the “e” of 3 and the “a” of 4, play at .3. A note played so quietly that it is felt more than heard is a **ghost note**. Sixteen steps, sixteen numbers: one strength for every step, rests included.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Your turn. Play the target first: the snare still hits five times a bar, but only two of them are loud.

<Editor exercise="ghost-the-snare" />

<OnPass>The backbeat stands out and the ghost notes fill the space around it: the same five hits, and the groove starts to breathe. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
By ear now, on the sixteenth grid. The hats play every eighth; listen for the kick that lands where no hat does.

<Choice exercise="find-the-sixteenth-push" />

<OnPass>A kick on an “e” or an “a” is the tightest kind of syncopation: a sixteenth off the eighths. Funk drummers put it there so the groove never sits still.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
The vamp again, stiff as a drum machine with no dynamics, and the Rhodes holding long chords. Give every part its strength, then swap in the guitar.

<Make exercise="your-funk-groove" />

<CanDo id="cd.code.ghost-notes" />

You can now give every hit of a groove its own strength and write a funk groove that breathes. Next: the push on paper, where a note that holds across the beat gets a curved line or a dot.

<Recap />
</Step>
