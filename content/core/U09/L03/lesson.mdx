<Step id="S1">
Verse and chorus take turns. Songs have other shapes too, and you have met one already in Ode to Joy: a section twice, something new once, then the first again. Hear a song built that way.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The sections here are named by letters: A is the first music you hear, B the next new music. Read the tune's plan before you count it.

<Predict exercise="count-the-a" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Run it and listen hard at bar 9.

<Example id="the-new-section" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Bar 9 leaves home: F, Em, Dm, then G, and the tune stops on 5 over V, waiting. At bar 13 A returns, and coming home sounds earned. A contrasting section that comes once, away from the song's home material, before it returns is the **bridge**. A A B A, four sections of eight bars each, is the shape of hundreds of standards from the 1920s and 30s, I Got Rhythm among them.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Play from the page. The lead sheet shows the whole song, its sections marked A, A, B, A. No roll this time: read the plan off the page.

<LeadSheet example="the-page" />

<Editor exercise="play-from-the-page" />

<OnPass>Twice A, once the bridge, A again: the tune now follows the page, and every part of the song agrees about where its sections start. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Name what you hear, section by section. Listen to the tune, not the instrument playing it.

<Choice exercise="name-the-form" />

<OnPass>A A A: one tune three times, on three different sounds. A song form that repeats the same music for every verse is **strophic**. When the Saints and Michael, Row the Boat Ashore are strophic: each verse brings new words to the same music.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Now tell two forms apart by ear: does the new music come once, or does it keep taking turns?

<EarDrill family="fam.frm.sections" level={1} trials={4} />

<OnPass>A bridge comes once, after the first section has played twice; a chorus keeps coming back. That difference is all you need to name most songs' shapes.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
The drums keep time and A is ready. The bridge sketch is plain on purpose: give it a tune that climbs or a new rhythm, keep it away from home, and let it stop on G. Then hear your song turn into a small standard.
