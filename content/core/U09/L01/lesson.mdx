<Step id="S1">
In Question & Answer you wrote four bars that ask and four that answer. Hear them as one piece of a song: once plain, once with a cymbal marking where each half begins.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The cymbal is one line of code you already know how to read. Read it before you count it.

<Predict exercise="read-the-crash" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Run it and watch the roll each time the crash hits.

<Example id="count-the-phrases" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Two four-bar halves, the same opening, a new ending: eight bars before it repeats. Beats group in fours inside a bar, and bars group in fours the same way. The first bar of each four feels strong, the way beat 1 of a bar does. That grouping of bars is **hypermeter**, and it is why you can feel a phrase start before anything new happens in it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Feel it in your hands. One tap a bar, on the kick, and say the bar's number out loud as you go.

<Tap exercise="tap-the-bars" />

<OnPass>One, two, three, four, then one again: each "one" landed as the tune started a phrase. Players count songs this way, in bars rather than beats.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
A crash on every bar makes every bar sound equally important. Mark only the strong ones.

<Editor exercise="mark-the-phrases" />

<OnPass>A crash on bar 1 and bar 5: four steps inside `< >` take four bars to come round, so the cymbal now says where each phrase begins. Two phrases, eight bars, one section. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Sections are built from phrases, and a song is built from sections. Here are two sections in a row, with no cymbal to help. Count in fours.

<Choice exercise="where-it-moves-on" />

<OnPass>Bar 9: new chords and a higher tune, on the first bar of a group of eight. Bar 5 starts a phrase, but it opens the way bar 1 did; bar 9 is where the song moves on to something else.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
The band plays eight bars. The four-bar question from the end of Question & Answer plays twice, so it asks and asks again. Give it an answer, and mark each phrase so a listener feels where it starts. Eight bars, a question and its answer, is how most songs you know open. Studio is open whenever you like: your C2 track can take a crash on each phrase too.
