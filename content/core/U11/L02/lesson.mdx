<Step id="S1">
Five Notes, No Wrong Ones gave you a bright scale to improvise on. Its dark twin is where rock and blues guitar lives. A short figure that repeats, under or over a song, is a riff; this one is six notes long, and a band could play it all night.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>The riff stops on C, a 3rd above home, so it leans into the next bar and keeps the loop turning. With the bass an octave down, the same six notes land with the weight of a band.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The riff's scale is the one from Five Notes, No Wrong Ones with one word changed: `minor` for `major`.

<Predict exercise="which-five" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Lay the riff's scale out in order and compare it with two scales you know.

<Example id="up-and-down" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>1 ♭3 4 5 ♭7, sung do me fa sol te: natural minor without 2 and ♭6, the minor pentatonic. Its letters, A C D E G, are C major pentatonic's five, started from A, the 6 of C. A minor is C major's relative, and the two pentatonics are relatives the same way.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
A riff that ends away from home makes you wait for the next bar. One that lands on home sits down.

<Editor exercise="land-it-home" />

<OnPass>The riff now lands on A, home, and the loop sounds settled. On this scale `n` 0 is home and `n` 5 is home an octave up.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The two pentatonics share their notes, but each has its own home, and home decides the colour.

<Choice exercise="major-or-minor-five" />

<OnPass>Bright or dark comes from the 3rd above home: 3 in the major pentatonic, ♭3 in the minor. A line that starts on ♭3 is still minor once you hear where home is.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Sing the five notes so the scale sits in your ear as well as in your fingers.

<Sing exercise="sing-the-five" />

<OnPass>Do me fa sol te do: the five notes behind a great many rock and blues riffs.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
This riff uses all seven notes of A minor; give it five, then let it become your own. A bass on the same notes an octave down makes it sound like a band, and a second bar that answers the first, as in Question & Answer, gives it somewhere to go.

<Make exercise="a-riff-of-your-own" />

<CanDo id="cd.compose.riff" />

In Five Notes, No Wrong Ones these five letters made a bright tune over C. Started from A, they now make a riff you wrote yourself and can land on home. Next: the notes that give the blues its bite.

</Step>
