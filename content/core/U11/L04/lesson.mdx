<Step id="S2">

<Step id="S3">
Run it with the chord band open. The last two bars play G7, then a plain C: hear the first one lean and the second one settle.

<Example id="the-pull" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>C, E, G and B♭: the C major triad with a ♭7 on top, ten half steps above the root. That is a **dominant seventh**. It sounds restless, as if it wants to move: G7 leans into C, and in the blues every chord leans like that.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now build one with your hands. Same recipe, on F.

<OnPass>F, A, C and E♭: F7. The ♭7 is a whole step below the octave, on a black key here. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Back in code. Play the target first, then turn the triads into dominant sevenths.

<Editor exercise="seventh-every-chord" />

<OnPass>C7, F7, G7, C7. One character a chord, and the polite loop now sounds like the blues.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
In code the 7 is in the name. By ear, listen for the lean at the top of the chord.

<Choice exercise="triad-or-seventh" />

<OnPass>Settled or leaning: that difference is the ♭7, and you can hear it now in any key and any voicing.</OnPass>
</Step>
