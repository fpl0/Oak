<Step id="S1">
A tune you know, slowed into a soul ballad: Aura Lee's verse on a tenor sax. First over its plain chords, the ones a transcription of it writes down, then over new ones. Follow the sax both times.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
A new chord works under a tune when the tune's long notes belong to it. Pick the chord before you hear the three side by side.

<Predict exercise="three-ds" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The new version on the page: the tune as it is written, with the new chords written above it.

<Example id="the-hook-on-the-page" views="code,staff" />

<OnPass>D7 is a secondary dominant: it leans into G7, a 5th below it. A♭ and B♭ are borrowed from C minor; they lean nowhere and change the light for half a bar each. The tune is the same in every bar. Putting new chords under a tune without changing the tune is **reharmonization**.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now by ear, on the verse's second phrase. Play the target first, listen to its bass, then change two chords.

<Editor exercise="borrow-by-ear" />

<OnPass>C, B♭, G, then C and F minor in one bar: I, ♭VII, V, I and iv. B♭ holds the tune's three Ds as its 3rd; F minor holds the long C as its 5th and lets the bar sigh before the loop comes round. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Five short loops, each with one borrowed chord. Find it, or name it when its bar is marked.

<OnPass>That is the ear the lab needs: a chord from outside the key, found by its colour and named by its bass.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The drums are in. The tune is fixed; the chords are yours.

<Make exercise="your-reharmonization" />

<CanDo id="cd.compose.reharmonize" />

<CanDo id="cd.hear.chromatic" />

You can now put new chords under a tune without changing it, and tell a borrowed chord from a secondary dominant by ear: you can use chords from outside the key. Next: the key itself moves, and stays somewhere new.

<Recap />
</Step>
