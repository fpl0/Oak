<Step id="S3">
Home, the new chord, IV and the sad IV, one a bar, so you can read every note.

<Example id="four-chords" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>E♭ holds E♭ and B♭, and only its G belongs to C major: it is C minor's chord, on the lowered 3rd, written ♭III. F minor is C minor's too. With ♭VI and ♭VII from the last lesson you now have all four: iv, ♭VI, ♭VII, ♭III, the chords of C minor inside a C major song. That mixing is called **modal mixture**.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
No code for the tune and no roll: only the page, its first chord, and four numerals. The key signature tells you where degree 1 is.

<Editor exercise="play-from-the-page" />

<OnPass>D, F, G, G minor: I, ♭III, IV, iv in D major. The tune's C natural sat inside the F chord and its B♭ inside G minor, the same shape as the riff in C. You read four bars of mixture off the page, a good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The numerals carry the borrowing: a flat on the root, and the case of the chord.

<OnPass>I, ♭III, IV, iv. The flat says the root is a lowered degree; the case says major or minor, as always.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Three ways into home. V pulls up; IV and iv both fall, and only one of them is minor.

<OnPass>V to I, IV to I, and iv to I twice. The last one is the plagal cadence with a minor IV, also called the minor plagal cadence: the close you first wrote with the sad IV, now with a name.</OnPass>
</Step>
