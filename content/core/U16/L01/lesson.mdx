<Step id="S1">
The last line of a slow song, twice. The first time every chord is the key's own. The second time one chord turns. You have heard this turn at the end of many ballads.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The only difference between the two loops is one letter in the chord string. Guess what it does to the chord before you hear it.

<Predict exercise="f-becomes-f-minor" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Here are the two chords on their own, stacked by hand so the roll shows each note.

<Example id="two-fours" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>F A C became F A♭ C: one note, down a half step. C major has no A♭, so F minor is not one of its chords. C minor has it: there, F minor is the chord on degree 4. A chord taken from the minor key on the same home note and used in major is a **borrowed chord**. This one is written iv, in lower case because it is minor.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now in code, in another key. Play the target first, then change one chord.

<Editor exercise="turn-the-second-one" />

<OnPass>G, C, C minor, G: I, IV, iv, I. The E in the C chord dropped to E♭, and the loop leans back home. You have turned a plain ending into the sad one, a good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The numeral does not change its number when the chord is borrowed. Only its case does.

<OnPass>I IV iv I. Same root, same degree 4; upper case for the key's own major chord, lower case for the minor one it borrows.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Your voice is analysed live on this computer. Nothing is recorded or sent.

The note that moves is the whole effect. In syllables it goes la, le, sol: degree 6, then ♭6, then 5.

<Sing exercise="la-le-sol" />

<OnPass>La, le, sol over IV, iv and I. Le does not belong to the key, and it does not stay: it falls a half step into sol, the 5th of the home chord. That fall is what you hear as the ache.</OnPass>
</Step>

The drums are in. Your loop is bright all the way through. Decide where it should ache.
