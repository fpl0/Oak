<Step id="S1">
Numbers are good for code. For singing, "seven" takes two syllables and "one, three, five" blurs together. Here every degree also gets a short word to sing, shown under its number.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The same line, the same numbers, in another key. Watch where the words go when home moves.

<Example id="hook-in-c" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Do stays on home, sol on 5, in C and in A alike. These singing names are **solfège**: one syllable for each degree, and because do moves with home, it is called movable do.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The seven syllables, in order from home: do re mi fa sol la ti, for degrees 1 2 3 4 5 6 7. After ti comes do again, the 1 above.

Do is always home, in every key on the circle. So do mi sol is 1 3 5 in C, in A, in E♭: the same sound, from a new home.

The numbers do not go away: they stay on the labels, and when you sing, numbers and syllables both count. In code nothing changes: do is `n` 0, sol is `n` 4.

<Example id="do-to-do" views="code,roll" />
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Your voice is analysed live on this computer. Nothing is recorded or sent.

<Sing exercise="sing-on-syllables" />

<OnPass>Do mi sol, ti up into do, re down into do. Syllables you can sing quickly, each one a degree.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
All seven degrees again, as in Leading Tone & Six, and now each one has a syllable. Sing the note on a syllable before you answer: your voice finds the way to do.

<Callout kind="tip">Do, mi and sol sound at rest. The others lean toward one of them.</Callout>

<OnPass>Every degree by ear, each with a name to sing.</OnPass>
</Step>
