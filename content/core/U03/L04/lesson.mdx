<Step id="S1">
Two degrees are still missing from your ears: 6 and 7, the top of the scale. Listen to the line climb through them to home.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">

<Predict exercise="stop-on-seven" hum />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Writing a climb one number at a time gets long. Strudel has a shorter way. <CodeRef fn=".." />

<Example id="run" views="code,roll" />
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
A range counts down as well as up. Keep the spaces around `..`: written as `0..7`, Strudel cannot read it as a range, and that part of the bar goes silent.

<Editor exercise="up-and-back-down" />

<OnPass>`[0 .. 7]`, then `[7 .. 0]`: sixteen notes in two words. A range fills the place of one note, so in a bar of its own it takes the whole bar.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The last two degrees by ear. Listen for which way the note wants to move.

<EarDrill family="fam.deg.fet-major" level={3} trials={6} />

<Callout kind="tip">7 sounds almost home already. If the note seems to strain upward, it is probably 7.</Callout>

<OnPass>All seven degrees by ear. 7 is the one that pulls hardest: it sits a half step under home and wants to rise to it. Degree 7 is the **leading tone**, because it leads you home.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Your voice is analysed live on this computer. Nothing is recorded or sent.

<Sing exercise="lead-them-home" />

<OnPass>Three resolutions: 7 up to 1, 6 down to 5, 2 down to 1. Each restless note found the stable one beside it.</OnPass>
</Step>
