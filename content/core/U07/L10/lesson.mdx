<Step id="S1">
The part of a song you hum on the way home is its hook. This one is a single bar, and it keeps coming back.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
What makes a tune stick? Hear two over the same chords.

<Choice exercise="which-one-sticks" />

<OnPass>Card A. It is short, it comes back, and it has room to breathe: a few notes, one high point, the same rhythm each time. Card B is just as correct over the chords and much harder to keep.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Each repeat of the hook is the same, except one note.

<Example id="now-and-then" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Two loops out of four. A `?` after a note plays it about half the time; which loops get it is chance, but the same chance every time you run the code. The hook stays itself, and its repeats stop sounding pasted.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
A hook is made by bringing an idea back. Here the idea is there in bar 1, and then forgotten.

<Editor exercise="tighten-it" />

<OnPass>Bar 1 in every bar, moved where the chord needs it. A hook can be this plain: the repetition is what makes it stick, and the moves keep it from standing still.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Three ways to let chance in: `?` on one note, `.degradeBy()` on the whole line, `.sometimesBy()` with a change of your choice. A little goes a long way.

<Editor exercise="let-chance-in" />

<Callout kind="tip">Try the amount at .5, then at .1. Somewhere between, the hook stays itself and still surprises you now and then.</Callout>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Hooks are made to be caught by ear. Count before you write: does it start on a 1?

<Editor exercise="catch-the-hook" />

<OnPass>A pickup on 5, a leap up to the high home, then a step down at a time. Two bars, one high point, and you could sing it after two hearings: that is a hook.</OnPass>
</Step>
