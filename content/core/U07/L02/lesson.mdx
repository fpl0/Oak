<Step id="S3">
The hook has one more function in it: `.scaleTranspose()`, after `.scale()`. Find out what it does from what you hear.

<Example id="one-idea-moved" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>One shape, 1 3 5 3, started on the root of each chord: degree 1, then 5, 6 and 4. `.scaleTranspose("<0 4 5 3>")` moves it that many degrees, a bar at a time, the way `.add()` moved a chord onto a new root. A short idea that comes back like this is a **motif**. A listener remembers it after one hearing, so every return sounds like the same tune.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The same motif over the drums, stuck in place. Make it climb.

<Editor exercise="climb-a-step" />

<OnPass>`.scaleTranspose("<0 1 2 3>")`: the motif one degree higher each bar. A motif repeated from another degree, so the same shape climbs or falls, is a **sequence**. Because the steps are degrees, the 3rds inside it change size to stay in the key: C E G, then D F A.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
A motif can also come back turned round. Here bar 1 asks, and bar 2 answers by retracing its steps.

<Editor exercise="turn-it-round" />

<OnPass>`[7 4 2 0]`: up in bar 1, back down the same way in bar 2. A motif played backwards, last note first, is a **retrograde**. `.rev()` does the same to a whole cycle: on a line that changes bar by bar, it turns every bar round.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Repeat, move, turn round: three ways to keep an idea alive. Hear what they add.

<Choice exercise="which-one-keeps-you" />

<OnPass>Repetition makes a motif stick. Moving it and turning it round keeps it interesting, and lets it fit each new chord: in card A, bar 2 starts on C against the G chord and bumps. Most good tunes do both.</OnPass>
</Step>
