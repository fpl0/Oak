<Step id="S1">
In By-Ear Transcription I this tune started on home, three times in a row. The real tune has one more note, before all of them. Listen for it.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Its first bar is three rests and a single note. The chords do not play in that bar at all.

<Predict exercise="when-first-note" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Count with the drums from the very start, out loud if you like.

<Example id="count-it-in" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>The low G plays on beat 4, alone, and leads into home on the next beat 1. A note or two before the first full bar, leading into it, is a **pickup**. In code it sits at the end of an extra first bar of rests, bar 0, so beat 1 of bar 1 still falls where the tune's first strong note does. Oak numbers bars from that first full bar, and calls bar 0 the pickup.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The same idea with two notes. The tune is written from bar 1; bar 0 is waiting.

<Editor exercise="write-the-lead-in" />

<OnPass>`[~ ~ 2 3]`: two rests, then degrees 3 and 4 climbing into 5. Without the rests the two notes would land on beats 1 and 2, and every strong beat of the tune after them would move.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Now by ear. Before you write anything, count: does the first note come on a 1?

<Editor exercise="where-does-it-start" />

<Callout kind="tip">If every note sounds right but the tune feels a beat off against the count, check where it starts.</Callout>

<OnPass>One note on beat 4, then a leap of a 4th up to the high home on beat 1. A pickup that leaps up into beat 1 is one of the most common ways for a tune to begin.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
On the page, the pickup comes before the first full bar: just its note and the rests before it. Bar 1 starts with the tune's first strong beat.

<Staff example="on-the-page" />
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
The drums keep time from the start; the chords come in on bar 1. Lead into the tune from bar 0, then make it yours.
