<Step id="S1">
Yesterday one chord leaned into V. A soul ballad does the same thing to other chords of the key, and each time the song arrives somewhere for a moment without leaving home. Hear the turn plain, then visited.

<Step id="S3">
The chord band reads each visitor by where it is going. The roll shows what each one is made of.

<Example id="hook-chords" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>A7 brings C♯, di, the leading tone of D, just as E7 brings si into A. Three visitors in eight bars, and G7 is the key's own. C7 is the odd one: its 3rd, E, already belongs to C major, and the note that makes home restless is its 7th, B♭, te, which falls onto the A of F.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Build one with your hands. The chord you want leans into A minor.

<OnPass>E, G♯, B, D: the key's own E minor seventh with G raised to G♯, the leading tone of A. It is written V7/vi and said "five-seven of six". A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The page gives you the tune and the numerals, and the key signature tells you the key. Read the accidentals: each one is a visitor's note on its way to the next bar.

<Editor exercise="play-from-the-page" />

<OnPass>G, B7, Em, G7, C, E7, Am, D7. D♯ came with B7 and climbed to E, F♮ came with G7 and fell to E, G♯ came with E7 and climbed to A.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Three loops, each with one visitor. Listen to where it lands.

<Choice exercise="aimed-at-which-chord" />

<OnPass>Si into A minor, di into D minor, te down into F. The note the visitor brings tells you where it is going.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
The drums are in. The ballad has three doors, each a chord a 5th above the chord after it. Open one, or all three.

<Make exercise="three-doors" />

<Callout kind="tip">When a visitor arrives, the tune's old note often rubs against it. Give the tune the visitor's note and the rub becomes a lean.</Callout>
