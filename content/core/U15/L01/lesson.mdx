<Step id="S1">
A singer rarely sings a tune exactly as written. On the way into a note they slide in from a half step away, and the line starts to sound like a record. Here is one tune twice: first plain, then bent.

</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The bent notes are written with the same numbers you know, plus a sign. Guess what this one plays before you hear it.

<Predict exercise="sol-something-sol" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The roll now names every note, including the four that are not the key's own. Each one sits a half step from a note of the key, and moves there next.

<Example id="hook-labelled" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Te falls to la, and ♯5 is `4#`. Each bent note is a **chromatic degree**: a note from outside the key, named by the degree it bends. A raised one leans up into the next degree, a lowered one leans down. In code the number is the step, as ever one less than the degree; the sign bends it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now bend two notes yourself. The B in bar 2 grinds against the chord; listen to what the target does with it.

<Editor exercise="fi-and-te" />

<OnPass>`3#` and `6b`: fi climbs back to sol, te falls to la, and bar 2 no longer grinds. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Chords set home, then one note. Name it, and let its pull help you: a bent note always wants to go somewhere.

<Choice exercise="bent-or-not" />

<OnPass>Fi leans up into sol, te leans down into la. These two come back in your warm-ups, joined later by me, le, ra, si and di.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Your voice is analysed live on this computer. Nothing is recorded or sent.

Two leans, one a bar. Sing them and you will feel where each note wants to go.

<Sing exercise="sing-the-two-leans" />

<OnPass>Sol fi sol, do te la. Fi pulled you back up to sol; te let you down onto la.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
The drums are in. The tune sits on the key's own notes; bend it the way a singer would.

<Make exercise="bend-a-tune" />

<Callout kind="tip">A bent note sounds best a half step from where it lands, on its way there. Bend a note that is about to move to its neighbour.</Callout>
