<Step id="S1">
A gospel pianist fills the space between two chords by walking the bass up into the next one, a half step at a time. First, last lesson's way into D minor. Then the walk-up, on an organ.

</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The chord that squeezes in over the climbing bass is one you have built before. Guess what it is before you hear it.

<Predict exercise="take-the-root-away" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Three chords, each stacked from its root so the roll reads bottom to top: the visitor you know, the chord from the walk-up, and the chord both of them lead into.

<Example id="three-stacks" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Three notes shared, C♯ E G: the two chords are nearly the same, and C♯, D's leading tone, is in both. C♯°7 is the leading-tone chord of D minor, a half step below it, so the bass climbs into D instead of falling. Leading into a chord with its own V7 or its own leading-tone chord, so that for a moment it sounds like home, is called **tonicization**. Home has not moved: two bars later C is home again.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now build one with your hands, aimed at G.

<OnPass>F♯, A, C, E♭: three half steps between each note and the next. F♯ is G's leading tone, the note that climbs into G. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Back to the walk-up. Play the target first, then fill the two bars that only wait.

<Editor exercise="two-more-pushes" />

<OnPass>`F#o7` into G7 and `G#o7` into Am7: the bass climbs F♯ G G♯ A, and each chord arrives a little like a home. If you wrote `Gbo7` or `Abo7`, they are the same keys; the sharp names say which way the root is going.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Both pushes do the same job. Your ears can still tell them apart, if you listen low.

<Choice exercise="which-push" />

<OnPass>V7, then the leading-tone chord twice. A V7 arrives with the bass falling a 5th; a leading-tone chord arrives with the bass climbing a half step.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
The drums are in. This turn waits two bars on each of its first three chords. Fill the waits, and let at least one of them climb.

<Make exercise="your-own-walk-up" />

<Callout kind="tip">A push does not need a whole bar. `[C C#o7]` holds C for two beats and climbs into Dm7 in the last two.</Callout>

You can now lead into a chord with its own diminished seventh and hear it from the climbing bass. Next: a dance band of 1925, horns and a trumpet, and dominants that lean into dominants.
