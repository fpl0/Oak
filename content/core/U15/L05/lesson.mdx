<Step id="S3">
Under the guitar and the horns, a chain has a thread you met in Seventh Chords: the 3rd and the 7th of each chord. Here they are on their own, over the bass.

<Example id="thread" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>One half step at every change, in both voices. G♯, the 3rd of E7, slides to G, the 7th of A7; the 7th of E7, D, slides to C♯, the 3rd of A7. The guide tones trade places at each link while the roots walk the circle, E A D G, and that slide is why a chain sounds as if it were rolling downhill.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Players name this chain by its roots. Oak's numerals name each chord by its job, so each link says where it is going.

<OnPass>V7/vi, V7/ii, V7/V, V7, I: every chord the V7 of the next, and only the last two belong to the key. On a chart you will also see III7 VI7 II7 V7; it is the same chain, named by where each chord stands. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
On the record Sweet Georgia Brown holds each link for four bars, which is what makes its chain feel so patient; here it is two bars a link, in its own key, F. Play the target first.

<Editor exercise="sweet-georgias-chain" />

<OnPass>D7, G7, C7, F. Each link leans a 5th down into the next, and the chain lands on home in bar 7. Worked backwards it is the same question each time: what is the V7 of the chord after me?</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The chords of a chain leave the key, but its bass does not. Listen to the bottom only.

<OnPass>1, 3, 6, 2, 5, 1: do, mi, la, re, sol, do. After the first move, 1 up to 3, each root is a 5th below the one before it; the bass here climbs a 4th to reach some of them, the same note an octave higher. All six are the key's own notes; what leaves the key is the 3rd of each chord above them.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
The drums are in. Your turn already walks the right roots; it only needs to lean. Turn the walk into a chain, then give the horns their stabs: copy the chords line, label it `horns`, sound it with `horns` and give it a `.struct()` of your own.

<Make exercise="your-own-1920s-turn" />

<Callout kind="tip">When a chord turns into a dominant seventh, the trumpet's note on it may rub. Raise it to the new note: `4#` over E7, `0#` over A7, `3#` over D7.</Callout>
