<Step id="S1">
From here on you will meet these chords the way a player does: on a record, with nothing on the screen. The standard you are taking down leans out of its key in just this way. Today you catch each lean by ear and name it by where it goes.

</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The method has three moves. Hear the note that does not belong. Name it: fi, si and di lean up, te leans down. Follow it into the next bar, where the chord it was aimed at arrives. Here is the first move, in loops Oak makes up for you.

<Choice exercise="find-the-bar" />

<OnPass>Each time, the bar that leaned sat right before a bar that sounded like an arrival. That pairing is what you listen for: a chord a shade outside the key, then the chord it was pointing at. Oak will bring these loops back in your warm-ups.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Now the second and third moves. The bar is given; the question is where the visitor points. Name the outside note, then follow it.

<Choice exercise="name-the-visitor" />

<OnPass>Si into la in G: the V7 of vi, B7 into E minor. Di into re in F: the V7 of ii, D7 into G minor. Te down to la in B♭: the V7 of IV, B♭7 into E♭. One note, one direction, one target. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The opening of the ballad from the start of the lesson, to take down. The bass first, as you have done since Chord Dictation Workshop; then the numerals, with a slash where a chord is aimed somewhere other than home.

<OnPass>Bass 1 6 2 5; I, V7/ii, ii, V7. The bass gave you every root. A on degree 6 would be the key's own A minor, but this chord was major with a 7th, its C♯ rose into the D of bar 3, and so it is the V7 of ii.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Your voice is analysed live on this computer. Nothing is recorded or sent.

The two visitors of the ballad each bring one note. Sing each note and the step it takes, and the lean is in your own voice.

<Sing exercise="sing-them-home" />

<OnPass>Di to re, a half step up; te to la, a half step down. The first is the C♯ of A7 resolving into D minor, the second the B♭ of C7 resolving into F.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The drums are in. This is your staircase from Seventh Chords, or Oak's copy if you would rather start fresh. Every chord in it belongs to the key, which is why it never leans. Try a door in front of each chord, listen, and keep the two that land best.

<Make exercise="open-two-doors" />

<Callout kind="tip">A door does not need a whole bar. `[Em7 C7]` gives Em7 two beats and the door two more, and the staircase keeps its shape.</Callout>

You can now find a secondary dominant by ear and say which chord it points at, in any major key. Next comes the other way to colour a key from outside: chords taken from its minor, starting with the saddest chord in pop.

</Step>
