<Step id="S2">

<OnPass>B, D and F hangs in the air. It wants to go somewhere, and in the hook it went to C.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
That restless chord is the stack on degree 7, the one triad of the key you have not looked at closely. Here it is, followed by where it leans.

<Example id="seven-then-home" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Three half steps, then three more: two minor 3rds. Two minor 3rds make a **diminished triad**. Its outer notes, B and F, are six half steps apart, the tritone, one half step short of the 5th that major and minor triads share. Stack two major 3rds instead, as in bar 2 of the hook (C, E, G♯), and the 5th grows to eight half steps: an **augmented triad**, bright but unsettled, as if it is still rising.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
In the hook, bar 2 was C major with one note raised. Build that chord yourself, under a tune that already climbs.

<Editor exercise="make-it-rise" />

<OnPass>C, E, G♯: two major 3rds. The raised 5th keeps its letter, G, and takes a sharp, and it rises on to A in the next chord, the way the tune does.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
All four colours now. Settled first or restless first: whichever way you sort them, start with that question.
