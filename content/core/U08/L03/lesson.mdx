<Step id="S1">
The doo-wop loop with a root bass, twice. The second time, one note in every bar is different. Listen to where the bass seems to be going.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Read the end of bar 1 before you hear it. The bass is in note names here, so you can see each step.

<Predict exercise="into-the-a" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Run it and watch the end of each bar on the roll.

<Example id="just-before" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Each changed note sits on beat 4 and is a step or a half step from the root that comes next, so the line leans into the new chord. That note is an **approach tone**. B comes down into A from the step above; E slides up into F and B into C by a half step, both in the key; F♯ slides up into G from outside the key.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
One change to finish the loop. The bass sits on G through bar 4; give the loop's return to C a lead-in.

<Editor exercise="lead-it-home" />

<OnPass>B into C: the half step below, in the key, the same pull as 7 rising to 1 in a tune. The loop now leans into its own start. A good place to stop for today if you need to.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Above, below in the key, or a half step below from outside it: each has its own colour. Tell them apart by ear.

<Choice exercise="which-side" />

<OnPass>From above, the line falls into the new root; from below, it rises. The half step from outside the key pulls hardest: one key away, on a note the key does not have.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
A new loop: C, F, C, G, the shape of a soul vamp. The bass sits on each root and leaves beat 4 empty. Fill some of those gaps with approach tones, from whichever side you like, and keep every new root on beat 1.

<Make exercise="lean-into-the-changes" />

<CanDo id="cd.compose.approach-tones" />

Any bass you write can now lean into its next chord. Next: one rhythm shared by the bass and the kick drum, so the two play as one.

<Recap />
</Step>
